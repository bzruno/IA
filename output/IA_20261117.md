## 6. Amazon and Google will together own nearly 30% of Anthropic after its IPO

- **Link:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/amazon-and-google-will-together-own-nearly-30-of-anthropic-after-its-ipo/

Anthropic on Tuesday disclosed more details about the biggest investment agreements with major cloud providers Amazon and Google.

While we already knew that Amazon stood to make a killing on its Anthropic investments from the IPO, Anthropic revealed that Amazon’s total stake will come in at just shy of 15% (14.7% to be exact).

Even more interesting is that Amazon could have, if it wished, owned an even bigger share of the company. When Amazon invested billions in Anthropic in 2023 and 2024 (long before Claude Code took the enterprise world by storm, back in the days when Anthropic looked like it would forever be a speck in OpenAI’s shadow), the cloud giant obtained promissory notes that could convert into equity.

These notes carry conversion prices of about $2.09 or $5.05 per share, depending on the year of investment. Amazon has opted to convert a chunk of them as part of the IPO into a special class of non-voting shares that will give it about 15% ownership. (The shares can receive dividends and convert to standard voting Class A shares when sold, the filing says.)

But it has also opted to hold on to billions of dollars’ worth of the notes for now. If it converted them all right now, it would own just under 24% of Anthropic (23.8% to be precise).

Google, meanwhile, will own just under 14% (13.7%), also in non-voting shares, which come from the conversion of notes, as well as the “private placement” sale of shares as part of this IPO. A group of major tech companies (AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, and TSMC Partners) have agreed to buy $26 billion worth of this non-voting stock. Google and Nvidia each agreed to buy $10 billion worth.

So while a lot of investors stand to make billions of dollars in Anthropic’s IPO, Amazon is one of the biggest winners, and that doesn’t even count what AWS stands to earn from Anthropic’s commitment to spend more than $100 billion on its cloud services over the next decade.

Tech’s wave of wealth has already reshaped Silicon Valley – notably in San Francisco – driving up demand for already scarce housing as young entrepreneurs and engineers became millionaires overnight. Now, that money is washing up on British shores too.

Anthropic, which employs about 5,000 people worldwide, has more than 200 employees in the UK. The company, founded in San Francisco in 2021, has announced plans to expand its presence in London, with a new office that is capable of accommodating up to 800 people.

Salaries across Britain’s AI sector are flying: OpenAI’s staff in London each received £2.3m on average last year – about 58 times the median UK full-time employee earnings. That puts them on par with partners at top law firms such as Linklaters and Clifford Chance, and ahead of some bankers in the City.

UK-based investors in these companies are also watching the value of their stakes grow at breakneck speed. Alex Gerko, the billionaire founder of the trading firm XTX and an early investor in Anthropic via the venture arms of the business, is expected to make hundreds of millions of pounds in paper gains when the company joins the stock market this week.

The flush of technology cash is already evident in London’s commercial property market. First, a wave of startups led to Shoreditch being nicknamed Silicon Roundabout, before tech firms began expanding across the capital. Now, US AI firms are expanding at pace in the UK, clustering tightly inside the King’s Cross “knowledge quarter” where Anthropic, OpenAI and Meta all work a stone’s throw from each other.

They have followed in the footsteps of Demis Hassabis, a totemic figure in AI’s breakthrough era who co-founded DeepMind in 2010 and sold it to Google for £400m four years later. He was one of the first to set up in the north London enclave. Isomorphic Labs, his AI drug discovery startup that is also now owned by the Google parent Alphabet, is in the area.

The AI gold rush is already being felt at the highest end of London’s housing market. Although tech bosses may be younger than the sector’s traditional client base, agents report that interest – while spread across London – includes plenty of familiar wealthy haunts.

For now, the AI boom’s physical footprint is unmistakable. In San Francisco, the average one-bedroom property now rents for $4,400 a month, up more than 25% from last year and nearly three times the US average. Landlords have been trying to capitalise on the boom, particularly in areas closest to the offices of the biggest AI companies including OpenAI and Anthropic.

Meanwhile in London, the transformation of King’s Cross from a railway goods yard once notorious for criminal activity into an international hub for tech and AI is well under way. Whether this new class of wealth is permanent, or a temporary flash in a historic tech bubble, is yet to be seen.

## 10. Microsoft says its new AI coding model rivals Claude Opus 5.5 at 5% of the cost

- **Fonte:** ZDNet AI
- **Publicado em:** 2026-11-17 19:42
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 7.0
- **Link:** https://www.zdnet.com/innovation/microsoft-ai-coding-model-rivals-claude-opus-5-5-ignite/

ZDNET’s key takeaways

- Microsoft unveiled three new AI models at Ignite 2026.

- It claims 96% of Claude Opus performance at 5% of the cost.

- Cheaper models could save Microsoft hundreds of millions.

At today’s Microsoft Ignite 2026 keynote, the company introduced two new frontier AI models built for what the company describes as “everyday” coding tasks. But there’s a bigger story: what these models might mean for Microsoft’s AI costs and its dependence on OpenAI.

Microsoft claims its MAI Code 2 model shows 96% of Claude Opus 5.5 performance at 5% of Opus 5.5’s price.

Microsoft says MAI Code 2 offers comparable performance to GPT-6.1 Sol or Opus 5.5 at a fraction of the cost. Microsoft’s Code 2 Flash model is comparable to the lower-end GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5, also at considerably lower cost.

This doesn’t mean that those of us who pay for $100/month subscriptions will suddenly get the equivalent for five bucks. Instead, these are usage-based metrics based on API usage.

Microsoft home-grown AI models could reduce its reliance on OpenAI and Anthropic

Throughout Satya Nadella’s keynote, Microsoft showed slides referencing a wide range of foundation models that are intended to work with Copilot and other Microsoft offerings. Microsoft famously has a multi-billion-dollar investment in OpenAI dating back to 2023, and has built many of its products and services around OpenAI intellectual property.

But now, Redmond is benchmarking its internally built models against Anthropic and OpenAI offerings, and also touting that it’s integrating these new models as optional choices for GitHub Copilot, Microsoft 365, and Fabric.

One thing that Nadella made clear in today’s keynote is how central AI is becoming in everything Microsoft does. Nearly all of those AI operations previously involved the company paying some token fees back to the frontier model providers or customers doing the same thing.

But with high-performing models of its own, Microsoft may become substantially less dependent on outside model providers, especially for the incredible volume of fairly basic and routine AI requests that occur across the company’s vast product line.

Microsoft turned its coding model into an AI worker for Office

If you think back over the history of agentic work models like ChatGPT Work and Claude Cowork, you may recall that they originated as agentic coding tools. As users adopted the coding harnesses in Claude Code and OpenAI Codex to off-label productivity applications, the AI companies noticed and packaged work-centric agentic tools that were more non-programmer-friendly.

There’s a lot of similarity between a coding agent and a work agent, including understanding a complex environment, using tools to change things, and producing reliable results. Coding turns out to be a very good environment for developing and testing these capabilities, particularly because code output is highly measurable. Coding agents are effective training grounds for non-coding work agents.

This becomes really interesting in today’s announcement. Along with Code 2 and Code 2 Flash, Microsoft announced MAI Work Flash, which it describes as having been derived from Code 2 Flash. Work Flash can help generate formulas in Excel, work with documents and instructions in Word, track decisions and extract useful information from Teams, and more.

Microsoft’s biggest AI model customer might be Microsoft itself

Think about the level of intelligence these tasks are demanding from the AI models. Not that much. As long as the models are good enough, they’ll be good enough.

If Microsoft can reduce its expenses and use reliable models at a much lower price, the company itself will be able to save quite a bit of money across its entire software economy, while at the same time maintaining a deeper level of vertical integration and, therefore, control.

Let’s run some hypothetical numbers. Let’s say a company is spending $0.01 per AI request, which, with these new models, can drop down to $0.001 per request.

A company with a daily inference cost of a million dollars (a company like Microsoft, for example) could drop that daily cost to about $100,000. Add that up across a year, and the change in models could save $325 million annually.

A million here, a million there. Pretty soon, you’re talking real money.

There’s no direct evidence that Microsoft is trying to produce replacement models for the frontier providers’ offerings. But it is an interesting and compelling story if we look at Microsoft developing its own engines for the software it sells.

Microsoft’s benchmark claims are impressive, but important questions remain

Microsoft says the numbers it’s reporting at are using a 50% introductory discount. That’s why I measured against $0.001 per request instead of $0.0005 per request above.

Benchmark selection could be cherry-picking. The numbers are impressive. But as we all know, there are lies, damned lies, and benchmark results. I’m fairly sure Microsoft isn’t padding these numbers too much, because it benefits from the new models more than even its own customers, but still. It’s an open issue.

The company also described clearly that these new models are not designed to handle the most challenging agentic and research workloads. You’re probably not going to use them to solve a decades-old mathematics challenge. But you’re very likely to use them to reformat your PowerPoint.

Speaking of reformatting your PowerPoint, Microsoft also emphasized that these new models did unusually well on visualization assignments. That’s a very good fit for not only UI design challenges but also graphics and slide production.

## 15. わが社はChatGPTでどう紹介されている？ OpenAIが企業向けの確認機能を提供開始

- **Fonte:** ITmedia AI+

ChatGPTがユーザーに向けて、自社をどのように紹介しているのかを把握したいと考える企業は多いだろう。OpenAIは、その把握に役立つ機能を企業向けにβ版で公開した。米国の対象企業であれば、自社のAIエージェントをChatGPT広告のリンク先に設定することもできる。

この記事は会員限定です。会員登録すると全てご覧いただけます。

OpenAIは2026年11月10日（現地時間）、企業が「ChatGPT」に自社のプロフィールを登録し、認証を受けられる「Business Hub」と、自社のエージェントを公開してユーザーと直接対話できる「Business Agent」を、「Businesses in ChatGPT」のβ版として発表した。

OpenAIは、Businesses in ChatGPTを、毎週ChatGPTを利用する約12億人と企業をつなぐ新たな手段と位置付ける。企業はBusiness Hubを通じて事業者として登録し、情報を管理するとともに、ユーザーとの接点に関するインサイトを確認できる。Business Agentは、対象となる米国のEコマース企業とオンラインサービス企業に提供される。

Business Hubが企業に示すのは、ChatGPT上のユーザーとの接点を集計した情報だ。ただし、企業に共有されない情報もある。その線引きは何か。

Business Hubは、ChatGPT上でのユーザーと企業の接点について、集計したインサイトを提供する。企業への言及数やWebサイトのクリック数のほか、ユーザーが自社を発見するきっかけになるトピックも確認できる。同トピックは、自社のWebサイトが引用された会話に共通するテーマだ。企業はこれらを、自社がどのように紹介されているかの把握や、共有する情報の改善点の発見に生かせる。

インサイトはユーザーのプライバシーを保護するよう設計されており、ユーザーの会話やBusiness Agentとのチャットを企業と共有することはないとしている。集計された形で提供され、会話ややりとりの数が一定の基準に達した場合にのみ利用できる。

Business Hubで登録と認証が完了すると、基本情報を更新したり、価格や在庫状況を含む商品フィード（商品情報のデータ）をセルフサービスで追加したりできるようになる。ユーザーは最も正確で最新の情報を基に、商品やサービスを検討できるようになるとしている。

対象は、ChatGPTが利用可能な地域に所在し、利用条件を満たす企業だ。OpenAIの既存のエンタープライズ顧客や広告主は、同社の担当者と直接連携して登録できる。Shopifyでストアを運営する事業者（マーチャント）は、Business HubまたはShopifyの管理画面からストアを接続することで、認証手続きを簡略化できる。Shopifyマーチャントの対象商品は、「Shopify Catalog」を通じて既にChatGPTに統合されているという。

Business Agentは、事業者登録を完了した対象の組織が構築、テスト、公開できるエージェントだ。ユーザーの質問に答え、自社の商品やサービスの検討を支援する。

ChatGPTによる回答に企業が表示されると、ユーザーはクリックしてその企業のChatGPTプロフィールを確認できる。その企業のBusiness Agentが利用できる場合は、エージェントとの会話を開始し、商品やサービスを検討したり、追加の質問をしたりできる。例えば商品を比較している人は、2つの選択肢の違いや、どの機能が自分のニーズに合っているかを質問できる。Business Agentは、企業の情報を基にこうした疑問の解消に役立つ。

Business Agentとの会話には、その旨が明確に表示される。ユーザーがそれまでChatGPTで行っていた会話とは別に扱われ、ChatGPTが独立して提供する回答とも区別されるという。

企業はBusiness Agentをスポンサー付きエージェントとして設定し、ChatGPT広告のリンク先にすることで、より多くの見込み客にリーチできるとしている。スポンサー枠は広告と明示され、ChatGPTの回答とは分けて表示される。

OpenAIは、今回の発表を、企業がChatGPTにより深く関わるための一歩と位置付けている。まずは、正確なビジネス情報、役立つインサイト、十分な情報に基づく意思決定を支える会話という基盤から始めるとしている。

ChatGPTが利用可能な地域で利用条件を満たす企業がまず使えるのは事業者登録と情報管理、インサイトの確認だ。顧客対応のエージェントは、米国の対象企業から始まる。
