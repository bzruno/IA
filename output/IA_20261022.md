## 1. NVIDIA Unveils Minerva to Bring End-to-End Observability for AI Factories

- **Fonte:** Nvidia
- **Publicado em:** 2026-10-22 12:00

- **Link:** https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/minerva-platform-infrastructure/

Launched today, NVIDIA Minerva is an AIOps platform built to help teams find problems earlier and keep AI factories productive. It brings together telemetry from infrastructure and AI workloads, then applies purpose-built AI models to turn that data into timely, actionable insight.

AI factories are among the most complex operations in the world, with vast numbers of GPUs working across high-speed networks to train models and serve AI applications. At that scale, a single degraded component can slow an entire job. The warning signs may be easy to miss, such as a GPU running hotter than its peers or an NVMe drive losing performance under load.

The platform focuses on AI factory yield, a measure of the useful work infrastructure produces through GPU hours, goodput, cluster uptime and token production.

Delivering a Dynamic View of an AI Factory

Traditional monitoring tools often divide the data center into separate views. Networking, compute, site reliability and machine learning teams each see a different part of the operation. Minerva connects those signals across NVIDIA GPUs, networking, storage, schedulers and workloads.

With these signals, Minerva builds a dynamic knowledge graph that logically maps the infrastructure — GPUs, SuperNICs, switches and cables — and the jobs running on it. This context helps operators trace a slow training job to the component affecting it, even across a factory with hundreds of thousands of GPUs.

It analyzes billions of data points per hour using supervised and unsupervised machine learning. It learns normal behavior, detects previously unseen anomalies, predicts emerging failures and correlates related events based on the factory’s topology. Instead of sending every symptom to an operator, it can group an event storm into a focused incident with a likely root cause.

Minerva comes with a native AI assistant and connects to external agentic AI tools. This adds a natural-language interface grounded in live telemetry. Operators can investigate cluster health and receive recommended remediation through conversation. Teams can keep a person in the loop to manually implement fixes or connect Minerva to automation through infrastructure application programming interfaces and external systems.

Results That Maximize AI Factory Yield

The ability to correlate workload and infrastructure issues along with its agentic AI capabilities help simplify AI factory operations. Instead of dealing with a blizzard of alerts, Minerva aggregates related problems into a single actionable item. This allows AIOps teams to move from reactive to proactive maintenance. And all of this helps AI factories maximize GPU uptime.

Minerva delivers value for the key metrics AI factories focus on:

- Longer time between interruptions: Minerva spots trouble before it happens, so jobs run longer without incident.

- Faster time to repair: When an incident does happen, Minerva pinpoints the problem, leading to a significantly quicker resolution.

- Greater insight: Modern AI infrastructure generates a flood of data; Minerva filters it out, leaving only the handful of alerts that actually matter.

IREN is among the NVIDIA partners using Minerva to optimize its AI factories.

“As we scale our AI factories, the key to providing the best service for our customers is maximizing the productivity of our GPUs,” said Denis Skrinnikoff, chief technology officer of IREN. “NVIDIA Minerva gives our operations team one view across the network, the GPUs and the jobs running on them, so we focus more on proactive maintenance rather than troubleshooting. That visibility strengthens our operating model for the infrastructure we’re building next.”

Joining IREN, Aolani, Bitdeer AI, IBM, Jio, Lambda, Quake AI and Tesla plan to deploy Minerva.

In addition to Dell Technologies, Lenovo and Supermicro will offer Minerva as part of their AI factory operations portfolios.

## 5. OlmoEarth v1.3: State-of-the-art embeddings for the whole planet

- **Fonte:** Hugging Face
- **Publicado em:** 2026-10-22 13:11
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 6.5
- **Link:** https://huggingface.co/blog/allenai/olmoearth-v1-3

🤗 Models | 📄 Tech Report | 📊 Data | 💻 Code | 🌎 Embeddings Explorer | 🗺️ Interactive story

Today, we’re releasing OlmoEarth v1.3, a state-of-the-art satellite embedding model. Alongside OlmoEarth v1.3, we’re sharing precomputed, annual embeddings for the whole planet (10m, 64- and 128-dimensions), plus a new browser-based tool to explore and customize maps based on those embeddings — the OlmoEarth Embeddings Explorer. This means anyone can put OlmoEarth v1.3 to work right away, from tracking change over time to finding similar places—no sign-in, and no advanced technical knowledge required.

Until now, the primary way to use OlmoEarth was fine-tuning in OlmoEarth Studio: training a model for each task and running it over satellite imagery to make a map. That requires GPU access and the ability to download and store the imagery. Embedding models compress satellite data into ready-to-use features that can be reused cheaply for many tasks.

With OlmoEarth v1.3, we’re excited to give users more ways to explore and engage with planetary artifacts in whatever way suits them best.

An embedding represents what the model learned from millions of images. OlmoEarth v1.3 reads a year of radar (Sentinel-1) and multispectral (Sentinel-2 and Landsat) satellite time series and produces one 128-dimensional vector for every 10-meter pixel on Earth; the first 64 dimensions also work on their own as a smaller embedding.

Foundation models and global embeddings are expensive to produce, and to make sure everyone is able to access the benefits, we’ve computed these embeddings for the entire planet and shared them with the community on Source Cooperative. From there, a crop map, a land-cover map, or a change analysis becomes a lightweight modeling problem: train a small classifier on top and run it locally.

How OlmoEarth v1.3 makes an embedding: a vision transformer reads the satellite data, an Attention Pooler pools it into one vector per patch of land, and a linear layer reduces it to 128 numbers.

We evaluated OlmoEarth v1.3's embeddings against AlphaEarth Foundations (AEF) and Tessera v2 across 27 evaluations, covering six continents and many different tasks. OlmoEarth’s 128-dimensional embeddings score highest on 20 of 27 evals and rank highest overall. The 64-dimensional version, at half the size, still leads on 18 of 27 evals. As part of this evaluation, we introduce PLANTEUR, a new crop-type benchmark for tropical farming, and the embeddings generalize best to places a classifier has never seen (PLANTEUR technical report is available here). Everything behind them is fully open: the model weights, the pretraining data, and the pretraining code.

In practice, this means a small classifier trained on the OlmoEarth embeddings is enough to map what’s on the ground: think classifying smallholder crops in Kenya, detecting brick kilns west of Dhaka, assessing fire damage in Indonesia, and tracking gold mining in the Peruvian Amazon.

Compared to previous generations of OlmoEarth models, OlmoEarth v1.3 was also our cheapest model to train. It took 53% of the computing time of our last version (v1.2) and 18% of our first (v1), with 50% and 22% of the carbon emissions. And it also remains a strong fine-tuning model, matching v1.2's performance across research and partner labels. Our latest model builds on the same foundation as earlier OlmoEarth versions, tuned so the embeddings stay lightweight without giving up accuracy across tasks, and that efficiency work carries forward into the versions ahead.

Full results and methodology are in our technical report.

If you wanted to reproduce it, pre-training OlmoEarth v1.3 Base would cost roughly $2,720 of rented H100 time for the final run. The weights, data, and code are all open.

How much each pixel’s embedding changed between 2017 and 2020 (left) and 2020 and 2025 (right). Magenta is the largest change. Left: mining spreading southeast, the big patch at lower right. Right: that corner has gone quiet; the change now sits in the older mining zone to the west, part regrowth, part renewed digging. Explore it yourself in the OlmoEarth Embeddings Explorer.

In Peru's Madre de Dios region, illegal gold mining around La Pampa peaked in 2017 and 2018, clearing more than 1,200 hectares of forest in the buffer zone of the Tambopata National Reserve, according to Amazon Conservation's MAAP monitoring program. In February 2019, Peru's Operation Mercurio shut most of it down. The question ever since has been, did it stay down?

The embeddings help answer that. Compare each pixel's vector across years, no model training required, and the 2017 to 2020 pair lights up where the mining was spreading to the southeast. Run the same comparison from 2020 to 2025, and the signal shifts. The crackdown is visible in the embeddings.

The more interesting part is what the second map still shows. It is not dark. According to MAAP, pockets in the northwest kept operating, and the old mining zone keeps changing as the land recovers. The embeddings register that too. The reprieve was brief, and the pressure is building again. In August 2026, the Associated Press reported, Peru sent navy patrols back into the reserve after prosecutors found mining had crossed into Tambopata itself.

Our interactive story opens on this stretch of Madre de Dios, and walks through several other examples from there.

The OlmoEarth Embeddings Explorer is an interactive analysis tool built from the embeddings themselves, rendered as a false-color map of the planet. Pan anywhere, flip between years to see how landscapes have changed, and pull up the underlying Sentinel-2 imagery to check the map against the real thing. Then start asking questions: click a place to find others that look similar, or drop a few labeled points and train a small classifier on the spot, then export the result as a GeoTIFF. No account, no setup required.

For many teams, testing an idea against OlmoEarth has meant a fine-tuning run, on the order of hours. In the explorer, a first pass takes seconds. You can analyze entire regions from a handful of labels, no GPU needed. When a task calls for fine-tuning, the embeddings can help you decide whether it deserves the compute, and where the model falls short.

The archive behind the Explorer holds embeddings at 10-meter resolution, roughly 150 TB per year, covering land and adjacent coastal waters. At launch, 2025, 2024, and 2023 are global, and 2017 to 2022 cover select countries, with more coverage on the way. You can retrieve the embeddings in the full 128-dimensional version or the lighter 64-dimensional version. Every embedding also comes with a record of where it came from. For each 20-kilometer tile and year, open provenance tables list the Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, and Landsat scenes the model read, with links to the public catalogs that host them, so you can trace any pixel back to the imagery behind it.

This release includes the OlmoEarth v1.3 Base model, the precomputed global embeddings, the OlmoEarth Embeddings Explorer, and the technical report. If your work needs a specific time window, OlmoEarth Studio runs the same model over the dates, modalities, and resolution that you choose, and a tutorial shows how to generate embeddings on your own compute. These tools work together: explore in the Embeddings Explorer, fine-tune in Studio when a task calls for it, and take your map from there. Everything is published under the OlmoEarth Artifact License, the same open license as v1 and v1.2.

## 7. Etsy bans AI-generated recipes and patterns from its online marketplace

- **Fonte:** CNBC Technology
- **Publicado em:** 2026-10-22 20:45
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 6.0
- **Link:** https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/22/etsy-bans-ai-generated-recipes-patterns-from-its-online-marketplace.html

Etsy said Thursday it will no longer allow merchants to sell AI-generated recipes and patterns on its online marketplace, as part of a wider push to set firmer rules around the use of artificial intelligence on its platform.

In a blog post, Etsy said it's banning the sale of "functional guides" that are created using AI, including patterns, cooking recipes and instructions. The products are usually marketed as digital downloads, such as crochet or sewing patterns. Users then "put in effort to achieve a finished product," the company said.

Sellers will also be required to show an actual item made using the instructions, not an AI-generated or simulated result, "so buyers know what to expect," Etsy said.

Etsy said in a separate notice to sellers that it was making the changes as AI threatens to erode buyers' and sellers' trust in the platform.

"Today, many active buyers say they can't tell which items involve AI-generated content," the company wrote. "It's become a top-three barrier to shopping more often on Etsy. And you, our seller community, have told us you're concerned about your work appearing alongside items that use AI in misleading ways, and that it's becoming harder for buyers to recognize your role in creating your items."

As part of the move, Etsy said it's barring sellers from creating bot-generated listings without any human involvement. Sellers will also be required to include at least one unedited photo of the actual item they're selling.

Etsy told sellers it will continue to allow AI-generated art on its platform as long as merchants "play a clear role in what they create and are transparent about the process."

"Sellers must still disclose when an item is created using AI and cannot misrepresent AI-generated work as handmade or entirely their own creation," the company added.

Founded in 2005, Etsy is known for its mix of craft makers and artisan goods. The company has been leaning more into that reputation as it has battled an influx of generic, mass-produced items from resellers and increasing competition from Amazon and Walmart, as well as newer e-commerce upstarts like TikTok, Temu and Shein.

Misleading, AI-generated products or images present a new challenge for Etsy and other online marketplaces. Etsy has in recent years struggled to curb the proliferation of AI-made crochet patterns and other AI "slop." Amazon recently required sellers to label photos or videos that feature AI-generated people in response to recent legislation mandating the disclosure.

While Etsy has cracked down on the use of AI in listings, it's been infusing the technology throughout more of its website. The company has used AI to build more detailed profiles of shoppers and surface relevant products in search results.

Etsy has also partnered with OpenAI on shopping features, including launching a ChatGPT app for product discovery.

## 8. Critical PHP web security flaws spark global alarm as Google, OpenAI and Amazon respond

- **Fonte:** CNBC Technology
- **Publicado em:** 2026-10-22 13:20
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 6.0
- **Link:** https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/22/php-google-openai-and-amazon-cyber.html

The tech world is racing to implement fixes after the discovery of critical security vulnerabilities in software used to power much of the internet.

The software, known as PHP, is an open-source programming language widely used to power websites and web applications. The discovery of the flaws prompted urgent warnings from governments worldwide, while tech giants including Google, OpenAI and Amazon have been involved in efforts to uncover the vulnerabilities, detect potential attacks and protect affected systems.

The two vulnerabilities, collectively known as "PHP2Shell," could allow hackers to remotely execute malicious code on affected servers, potentially exposing sensitive data, according to cybersecurity researchers. While not every website using PHP is necessarily vulnerable, the software's widespread use has raised concerns about the potential scale of attacks.

The U.S. National Security Agency on Wednesday urged organizations using PHP to prepare security updates addressing the vulnerabilities, warning that the flaws could provide attackers with direct access to server file systems and databases. The agency recommended taking affected systems offline until the vulnerabilities are fully addressed if immediate updates are not possible.

The Federal Bureau of Investigation also warned that malicious actors could exploit the vulnerabilities to execute code remotely, putting sensitive data and networks at risk.

Cybersecurity authorities in Singapore, the United Kingdom, Australia, Canada and the European Union have issued similar alerts, underscoring concerns about the potential impact on businesses, government agencies and other organizations that rely on PHP-based systems.

Amazon said its threat intelligence team had detected attempts to exploit the vulnerabilities before they were publicly disclosed, using its MadPot threat detection system, which monitors suspicious activity across internet-facing infrastructure. It said the findings suggest malicious actors were already attempting to exploit the vulnerabilities. However, Amazon said it remains unclear how the attackers obtained information about the flaws ahead of their disclosure.

Meanwhile, Google and OpenAI highlighted the growing role of artificial intelligence in identifying and addressing cybersecurity threats. Google said its cybersecurity unit Wiz used AI models to help find and investigate the vulnerabilities, while its Gemini AI contributed to efforts to assess the flaws, test potential attacks and verify security patches.

OpenAI said researchers working with its AI models had also helped uncover critical vulnerabilities in PHP. The company said it had spent the past two months leading a coalition of software maintainers, cybersecurity researchers and security providers to coordinate fixes and prepare protective measures ahead of public disclosure.

The ChatGPT maker described the flaws as among the most significant security issues in recent memory, highlighting both the potential of advanced AI systems to identify vulnerabilities in widely used software and the risk that similar capabilities could be exploited by cybercriminals.

Cloudflare, which provides cybersecurity and website infrastructure services, said it had deployed protections against the PHP2Shell vulnerabilities through its web application firewall. The company said its measures were designed to help block malicious requests targeting affected systems, providing an additional layer of defense as organizations work to install software updates.

The PHP development team has released security fixes, with authorities urging website operators to identify affected versions and apply the updates as quickly as possible. The episode underscores how vulnerabilities in widely used software can pose risks across industries and borders, even as AI increasingly plays a role in helping security researchers uncover and address those weaknesses.

## 11. Does Your Model Run on AWS Trainium? Look It Up

- **Fonte:** Hugging Face
- **Publicado em:** 2026-10-22 21:00
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 5.0
- **Link:** https://huggingface.co/blog/torch-neuronx-model-support

In a previous post, we introduced TorchNeuron, the native PyTorch backend for AWS Trainium (the torch-neuronx package). With it, Transformers runs its own modeling code on Trainium, as it does on any other device, so any Transformers model on the Hub may run. Which ones actually do?

The torch-neuronx model support Space answers that question for the most popular models on the Hub. It tells you whether a model runs unmodified on Trainium, and gives you the script to run it yourself.

The Space opens on the Selected view: our own selection of models picked among the top models on the Hub by downloads and by trending score. Each row gives the result, the number of Trainium cores the model is sharded over, the wall time of the run (compilation included) and the peak device memory.

To look up a given model, switch to the Trending or Downloads view and type part of its name. These views follow the Hub's own rankings, one task at a time, whether a model was tested or not. When a model was left out on purpose, the Space tells you why (most of the time it was not prioritized, sometimes it is truly incompatible). You can also pick the versions of the Neuron compiler (neuronx-cc) and of Transformers to look at.

At the time of writing, only Transformers models are tested, and our selection is limited to text-generation and image-text-to-text models. We plan to expand to diffusers, sentence transformers, and other transformers tasks.

Select a model to see how to run it. The Space lists the Neuron stack the model was tested on, the packages to install on top of it, and the run.py script that ran. The script follows the recipe of the TorchNeuron announcement: it loads the model with from_pretrained, sharded when it needs to be, then generates with a static cache and torch.compile(backend="neuron").

On a Trainium2 (trn2) instance with that stack, for example in the AWS Neuron Deep Learning Container, install the packages, then run:

python3 run.py

No need for torchrun: the script launches its own torch.distributed ranks when the model is sharded.

A first run starts by compiling the model's graphs. To skip that step, copy the graphs the same compiler built during the test, from the public NEFF cache bucket. The Space gives you the command, with the right compiler version:

hf buckets sync hf://buckets/aws-neuron/torch-neuronx-neff-cache/neuronxcc-<version> /tmp/neff_cache

Each test loads the model on a trn2 instance, sharded over the smallest number of Trainium cores that can hold its weights. Most models are sharded with tensor parallelism. Mixture-of-experts models without a tensor-parallel plan use expert parallelism only. The test then generates 32 tokens and checks them. On a single core, the tokens are compared with those of a CPU run of the same checkpoint. When the model is sharded, a small judge model checks that the text is fluent English.

At the time of writing, only models that fit on 4 Trainium2 devices are tested, which means 16 cores at most. A PASS says nothing about throughput, or about longer generations.

Everything the Space shows comes from the public aws-neuron/torch-neuronx-model-support dataset, with one Parquet file per pair of neuronx-cc and library versions.

Each row holds a model's result, the error when it failed, the versions of the whole stack and the run.py that ran. You can query it directly:

import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_parquet("hf://datasets/aws-neuron/torch-neuronx-model-support/data")

df[df.model_id == "Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B"][["neuronx_cc", "library", "library_version", "neuron_cores", "result"]]

The dataset card gives the exact meaning of each result.

Next time you wonder whether a model runs on Trainium, look it up.

## 14. Ensemble forecasting with IBM Granite time-series models

- **Fonte:** Hugging Face
- **Publicado em:** 2026-10-22 04:51
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 5.0
- **Link:** https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-research/granite-timeseries-ensemble-r1

Four open Granite models, one extensible forecasting interface, and less guesswork about which model to use.

Pre-trained time-series models can forecast without training on your dataset, but you still have to choose one. A model that handles smooth industrial signals well may struggle with noisy retail demand or a long seasonal horizon, and the best choice depends on the series and the task.

Granite Timeseries Ensemble reduces that choice to a sensible default. One recipe combines four complementary Granite architectures and returns median and quantile forecasts, with no retraining and no fitted ensemble weights. Start with the Probabilistic Ensemble (uniform probability pooling), compare the adaptive Inter Quartile Range (IQR) Ensemble, and extend the same interface with compatible forecasters. The ensemble recipe and its default Granite members are permissively licensed.

The idea will be familiar from weather forecasting. Ensemble forecasts explore a range of possible outcomes, and systems such as the North American Multi-Model Ensemble combine different models for seasonal prediction. Time-series forecasting benefits in a similar way: complementary models offer different views of what comes next, and an ensemble merges those views into one forecast.

Install Granite TSFM and load the recommended four-model recipe directly from Hugging Face:

pip install "granite-tsfm>=0.3.11"

import pandas as pd

from tsfm_public.models.ensemble.modeling_ensemble import QuantileEnsembleForecaster

df = pd.read_csv(

"https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zhouhaoyi/ETDataset/main/ETT-small/ETTh1.csv",

parse_dates=["date"],

)

ensemble = QuantileEnsembleForecaster.from_pretrained(

"ibm-granite/granite-timeseries-ensemble-r1",

)

result = ensemble(

df.tail(1024),

timestamp_column="date",

target_columns=["OT"],

context_length=1024,

prediction_length=24,

)

print(result.prediction_outputs) # point forecast

print(result.quantile_outputs)   # quantiles

The recipe stores the ensemble configuration rather than merged model weights. It downloads the four member checkpoints at runtime, so allow extra time for the first run. For the full configuration, member checkpoints, evaluation details, and limitations, see the model card.

result.prediction_outputs contains the point forecast, while result.quantile_outputs contains the quantile predictions which by default range from  0.1 to 0.9. Applications can use the point value as a central prediction and selected quantiles to express forecast ranges. For example, the 0.1 and 0.9 quantiles define a central forecast interval with nominal 80% coverage.

The default recipe combines four pre-trained Granite models with different architectures:

For background, see the PatchTST-FM-r1 paper, PatchTST-FM-r2 architecture overview, FlowState paper, and Tiny Time Mixers paper.

These architectures capture trends, seasonality, and local variation in different ways, so their errors need not coincide. A 50-year review of forecast combinations describes how combining forecasts can reduce the risk of picking the wrong single model, while stressing that member quality and diversity still matter.

Each member returns quantile forecasts. The two main recipes use different rules to turn those forecasts into one predictive distribution and its median.

The Probabilistic Ensemble (uniform probability pooling) gives each member equal influence, pools their quantile forecasts in probability space, and extracts the requested quantiles from the combined values. Because the weights are fixed, there is no fitting step and no weighting hyperparameters to tune for a new series.

The forecast combination puzzle offers useful motivation: in point forecasting, simple equal-weight combinations have often held up well against methods with estimated weights. This recipe applies the same equal-influence idea to probabilistic forecasts. It is the default loaded in the example above.

The IQR Ensemble weights members according to their forecast spread, giving narrower intervals greater influence. It approximates each member’s interquartile range from the closest available quantiles around the 25th and 75th percentiles. Weights vary by sample, target, and horizon step, and like the Probabilistic Ensemble, nothing is fitted from historical forecast errors. Isotonic regression keeps the combined quantiles from crossing.

A narrower interval does not necessarily mean a more accurate or better-calibrated forecast, and IQR weighting can favor an overconfident member. If you want to experiment, the recipe exposes a temperature setting (higher values move the weights toward uniform) and an optional cap on any single member's weight.

Load the evaluated IQR recipe by changing only the subfolder:

"ibm-granite/granite-timeseries-ensemble-r1",

subfolder="recipes/granite-iqr",

)

The default recipe uses four Granite models, but the common Forecaster interface also accepts compatible external models. You can change the member mix or the aggregation method and keep the same downstream forecast format, with no merging or retraining of member checkpoints. That makes it easier to fit the ensemble into an existing forecasting stack, and to add forecasters suited to your domain as your needs evolve.

The repository also includes expanded recipes that add IBM Research checkpoints. These carry separate license terms, including non-commercial and research-use restrictions, so review each member's model card before using them.

For any new combination, check forecast compatibility, accuracy, calibration, latency, and licensing.

GIFT-Eval is a broad benchmark, but aggregate gains don’t mean every series benefits, and average ranks don’t establish calibration. The way to find out how the recipes behave for your application is to backtest them on representative data:

- Match your use case. Use historical forecast windows that reflect the data available at each forecast origin, the horizon you need, and the series you expect to serve.

- Compare like with like. Run the Probabilistic Ensemble, the IQR Ensemble, and a strong individual member (such as PatchTST-FM-r2) on the same windows.

- Look beyond point error. Assess quantile loss alongside MASE, and check the empirical coverage of the 0.1 to 0.9 interval against its nominal 80%.

- Measure the cost. Record runtime and memory use to judge whether the benefit of combining four models justifies it for your workload.

Keep two limitations in mind. The current recipe forecasts multivariate channels independently, without using cross-channel information, and it loads four member checkpoints, which increases inference time and memory relative to a single member. Member context limits, horizons, preprocessing, and missing-value handling also differ, and neither aggregation method guarantees calibration or robustness under distribution shift, so keep monitoring forecasts after deployment.
