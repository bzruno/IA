Fast and cost-effective at scale

You can also specify duration in one-second increments from 3 to 15 seconds, giving you room to tell complete stories, create product explainers, or produce full social ad spots in a single generation.

You can guide generation combining character, product, or style images, short motion clips (up to three seconds), and audio cues — and Omni 1.2 Flash holds onto those details from start to finish. Faces, garments, packaging typography, and brand colors stay consistent across camera angles, making it easy to turn existing product photography or character sheets into virtual try-on clips, catalog spins, and branded video ads.

Combining one model photo with front-and-back product shots of three outfits, Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash generates a 15-second virtual try-on sequence across 360-degree turns and match cuts while keeping the model’s identity and each garment’s cut, drape, and back details consistent.

You can maintain stronger character, object, and scene consistency from the first to the last frame, with less visual drift or morphing across motion and camera transitions. Whether you’re interpolating between a first and last keyframe, or extending a clip to build a longer sequence, products, characters, props, and environments stay consistent from shot to shot.

You can refine the same clip turn by turn — such as swapping a product or background, changing the lighting or weather, or switching the aspect ratio — instead of starting over from scratch. Multi-turn conversational editing works across full 15-second clips, making it easy to produce ad variants for different audiences and placements without re-briefing or re-generating.

Starting from one generated clip, multi-turn conversational prompts swap the background turn by turn — from a striped pavilion tent to an oak forest, a stable with a white horse, a market fair, and a stone castle — while keeping both characters’ performances, armor reflections, and camera framing intact.

More natural speech

You can generate scenes with more fluid, natural-sounding dialogue, clearer lip-sync, and distinct multi-speaker conditioning. More controllable, cinematic look

Longer clips with smoother motion and tighter consistency

## 2. OpenAI、サイバーセキュリティ特化型「GPT-6 Cyber」発表 Firefoxの未知の脆弱性81件を発見

- **Fonte:** ITmedia AI+
- **Publicado em:** 2026-11-05 19:57
- **Idioma:** ja
- **Relevância:** 9.0
- **Link:** https://www.itmedia.co.jp/news/article/2611/06/2000002931/

米OpenAIは11月5日（現地時間）、サイバーセキュリティに特化したAIモデル「GPT-6 Cyber」を発表した。汎用モデル「GPT-6 Astra」をベースに、脆弱性の発見や攻撃手法の検証などの能力を強化したモデルで、同社の防御者向けプログラム「Daybreak Red」の承認ユーザーに限定して提供する。

GPT-6 Astraは、9月に発表されたGPT-6ファミリーの最上位モデルで、高度な推論やソフトウェア開発、科学研究などに対応する汎用モデルだ。一方、GPT-6 CyberはAstraをベースに、実際に動作する攻撃コード（エクスプロイト）の開発やWebアプリへの侵入テストなど、より高度なセキュリティ研究を目的とする。強化学習によってサイバー攻撃の再現や脆弱性の探索能力を高めるとともに、正当な研究目的の依頼を過度に拒否しないよう調整した。

OpenAIによると、GPT-6 CyberはAstraを上回るサイバー能力を持つ。既知の脆弱性を悪用できるかを測る「ExploitGym」では48.5％を記録し、前世代の「GPT-5.6 Cyber」の32.1％から約51％向上した。ソースコードを参照しないブラックボックス環境でのWeb脆弱性探索においても、Astraの32.0％に対して40.3％を記録した。

実際のソフトウェアを対象とした研究では、Firefoxで未知の深刻度の高い脆弱性81件を発見し、Linuxカーネルでは948件の脆弱性について実際に動作するエクスプロイトを開発した。Mozillaには修正案も提供しており、既に多くの脆弱性が修正されているという。CiscoやCodeRabbitなどのセキュリティチームも同モデルを活用している。

開発の背景には、高度なサイバー能力を持つAIが攻撃者にも悪用され得るという懸念がある。OpenAIは7月に、社内のAIエージェントが許可された範囲を超えてHugging Faceのシステムに侵入した事案を公表した。8月には、サイバー能力が最上位の「Critical」に達する可能性を理由に、Astraの一部開発を一時停止している。

今回公開したシステムカードでは、モデルが指示された範囲を逸脱しないかについても評価している。許可されていない別のシステムを攻撃するかを調べるテストでは、GPT-5.6 Cyberが5.51％の割合で逸脱したのに対し、GPT-6 Cyberは0.07％に低下。運用時の安全指示を加えると、評価中の逸脱は0％だった。

一方、安全性の課題も残る。タスクを中止すべき警告に適切に従った割合は73.0％にとどまり、Astraの78.38％を下回った。また、タスクを完了できなかった理由を最終回答で十分に説明できないケースも報告されており、OpenAIは改善を続けるとしている。

GPT-6 Cyberはまず、審査を通過した一部のDaybreak RedユーザーにOpenAI APIと「Codex」で提供する。その後、対象をDaybreak Redの全ユーザーに拡大する予定だ。利用には本人確認などが必要で、組織には多要素認証やシングルサインオン（SSO）などのセキュリティ対策を求める。一般のChatGPTユーザー向けの提供予定は発表されていない。

OpenAIは、発電所や水処理施設などの重要インフラを守る組織向けにも専用のアクセス枠を設ける。高度なAIによる攻撃が広がる前に、防御側が脆弱性を発見・修正できるようにする狙いだ。

## 3. The Wizard of Open Weights: Chris Alexiuk Makes AI Easy With Open Models

- **Fonte:** Nvidia
- **Publicado em:** 2026-11-05 13:00
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 8.0
- **Link:** https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-life-chris-alexiuk/

Chris Alexiuk has a simple rule about open source AI: If you believe in it, be loud about it.

“We should be shouting about this technology from the rooftops,” said Alexiuk, a Toronto-based senior product research engineer in deep learning at NVIDIA who specializes in making open large language models (LLMs) practical for working developers.

Alexiuk’s preferred “rooftop” is YouTube, where he’s built a following teaching curious thousands how to build, fine-tune, evaluate and ship production LLM applications and agents.

“I think the people who will most help us understand how to build and apply AI likely haven’t even heard of it yet,” he said. “Trying to get knowledge and information out to as many people as humanly possible is what continues to drive me.”

Bringing NVIDIA Nemotron to Developers Everywhere

Having spent years teaching developers to build with open models, Alexiuk has concluded that transparency isn’t a feature of good AI; it’s the precondition for it.

“Fully open source AI is necessary for the democratization of intelligence,” he said. “You should have access to the weights, the data that went into it, the training recipes, everything.”

That’s why, on his LinkedIn profile, Alexiuk styles himself “No. 1 Nemotron Fan,” referring to the NVIDIA Nemotron family of open source, multimodal models built for long-running, self-evolving and specialized AI agents.

“I think Nemotron is the most important project that exists,” said Alexiuk, who also uses the pseudonym “Joe Nemotron” across social media. “I’m a firm believer that openness is a very important thing to undertake, so I’m going to be the No. 1 fan of whoever is doing that the best. And that is Nemotron.”

It’s this matter-of-fact directness and affable approachability that makes Alexiuk’s teaching style so appealing. On the social media platform X, he goes by the handle “llm_wizard.” His avatar: a laughing cartoon caricature sporting a slouchy sorcerer’s hat and brandishing a magic wand.

It’s no surprise, then, that Alexiuk likens Nemotron to a magical library — its users fellow wizards in search of knowledge that can move mountains.

“You’re a wizard in search of prepared spells, and Nemotron has a spell for everything,” he said. “Just choose the one you need.”

In non-wizard terms?

“There’s a category of model that’s going to be close to the kind of work you want to accomplish,” Alexiuk said, explaining that Nemotron supports that whole ecosystem of AI users: from individual developers using a model like Nemotron 3.5 Lightning for repetitive, high-frequency execution work to a company deploying Nemotron Ultra for long-running autonomous agents, deep research and enterprise tasks.

“I think that openness helps more people understand AI and hopefully leads to more people doing really ingenious things with these tools at the limit,” Alexiuk said. “If a tool is amplifying skill, it’s amplifying human expression.”

From Birding to Model-Building

Alexiuk first became fascinated with machine learning in a computational physics class at Trent University in Ontario, Canada.

“After that, I learned about TensorFlow and that you could build image classifiers that ran on your GPU,” he recalled. As an initial one-off project, Alexiuk built a classifier that would alert him whenever his friend’s distinctive pet bird, a Yellow-Faced Gray Budgerigar, appeared in frame on his webcam during shared video game sessions.

“Because we were playing video games, I wasn’t focused on my friend’s webcam, but I didn’t want to miss seeing the bird when it appeared,” Alexiuk said. “And then my whole life changed, because in that instance I realized it’s crazy what you could do with technology.”

Since then, he’s been embedding himself in the open source AI community.

“To imagine within the span of a few years going from freaking out because I could know my friend’s bird was in the camera frame to being able to make any website in a matter of minutes with a model that’s running on my laptop — I cannot wait to see what’s next,” Alexiuk said. “It’s never been more accessible to get into and learn about AI.”

And if another LLM wizard arises to challenge him for the title of No. 1 Nemotron Fan?

“It would be the greatest day of my life to be surpassed by an even bigger fan of Nemotron, who had heard about Nemotron through the work I was doing,” Alexiuk said. “That would be a capstone to my career that I can only dream of.”

Hear more from Alexiuk by following the NVIDIA Nemotron Labs Livestreams series on YouTube.

## 5. OpenAI、データセンター立地地域への約束と政策提言を発表 電力費の全額負担や公職者とのNDA不使用など

- **Fonte:** ITmedia AI+
- **Publicado em:** 2026-11-05 19:03
- **Idioma:** ja
- **Relevância:** 7.0
- **Link:** https://www.itmedia.co.jp/news/article/2611/06/2000002928/

米OpenAIは11月5日（現地時間）、AI向けデータセンターの建設・運営に関する地域社会への約束「National Community Compact」と、米国の連邦、州、地方政府に向けた政策提言「Compute Campus Policy Blueprint」を発表した。電力と関連インフラの費用を全額負担することや、公職者との秘密保持契約（NDA）を原則として結ばないことなどを盛り込んだ。

National Community Compactは、OpenAIが今後所有・運営するデータセンター（同社はデータセンターの呼び方を「Compute Campus」に変更した）に適用するものだ。施設ごとに地域との協定を設け、計画の初期段階から住民の意見を反映させ、取り組みの進捗を毎年公表する。

主な約束は、電力と必要なインフラの費用を全額負担し、計画の遅延や中止による費用を他の電力利用者に転嫁しないことだ。水の使用を抑えた冷却方式を優先し、施設ごとの電力・水使用量や第三者による騒音調査の結果も公開する。公職者とのNDAは、セキュリティ情報や企業秘密などの保護に必要な場合を除いて結ばない。地元住民の雇用や職業訓練も支援する。

地域への投資例として、ジョージア州エフィンガム郡では480万ドルの助成により、郡内の児童・生徒約1万4000人に無料の学校給食を提供する。オハイオ州パイク郡では、OpenAIとSB Energyが保育支援に計600万ドルを拠出する。

一方、Compute Campus Policy Blueprintでは、企業の自主的な取り組みだけでは不十分として、政府に業界共通のルール作りを求めた。大口電力利用者に送電網への追加負担を求める一方、電力需要の逼迫時に消費量を削減できる施設には送電網への接続を容易にするよう提言した。エフィンガム郡では、電力会社が需要のピーク時にOpenAIへの供給を最大1000MW削減できる契約を結んでいるという。

このほか、電力・水使用量や雇用実績の情報公開、公職者にNDAを求める場合の制限、税優遇を受けた企業が約束を守らなかった場合の優遇分の回収なども提案した。

OpenAIは1月に、データセンター立地地域への責任を定める「Stargate Community」を発表しており、今回の取り組みはその延長に当たる。

米国ではAI向けデータセンターの建設急増に伴い、電気料金の上昇や水資源への影響を懸念する住民の反発が強まっている。11月3日の中間選挙では、データセンター規制を掲げた候補者が相次いで勝利した。米Washington Postによると、少なくとも38の地域で建設の禁止や制限を支持する投票結果となった。OpenAIの発表では、この選挙結果には触れていない。

9月には米NVIDIAや米Googleなどが、電力需給に応じて消費量を調整するデータセンターの普及を目指す「AI Energy Management Alliance」を設立している。米Amazonも10月に電気料金への配慮や政府機関とのNDA不使用などを掲げる「Amazon Data Center Commitment」を発表した。

San Francisco (EUA) | Reuters As vitórias expressivas dos democratas nas eleições de meio de mandato nos EUA nesta semana foram interpretadas por muitos como um repúdio dos americanos aos planos da indústria de tecnologia de construir data centers por todo o país. Diante disso, a OpenAI deu início a novas estratégias para tentar mudar a opinião pública.

O laboratório de IA apresentou nesta quinta-feira (5) um conjunto de diretrizes —em elaboração desde agosto— destinadas a ajudar a empresa a construir mais confiança com as comunidades locais e convencê-las de que os data centers trarão benefícios a elas.

A OpenAI afirmou que não utilizará mais acordos de confidencialidade com autoridades públicas ao negociar ou discutir futuros locais de instalação, embora informações proprietárias sigam sob sigilo. A empresa afirma que também garantirá aos moradores acesso aos planos de construção e aos seus potenciais impactos sobre o trânsito, o ruído e a iluminação.

"Esses projetos não são o tipo de data centers em que as pessoas historicamente pensavam —uma espécie de caixa-preta em um terreno vago que trazia pouquíssima utilidade econômica para as comunidades onde estava inserida", afirmou ele a jornalistas.

OPOSITORES DOS DATA CENTERS AVANÇAM NAS URNAS

Além disso, das 41 propostas submetidas a votação direta sobre o tema nesta semana, todas —com exceção de uma— resultaram em derrotas para os data centers, de acordo com o site especializado Ballotpedia.

As ações de operadoras de data centers e de empresas de energia, que estavam entre as maiores beneficiadas pelo boom de investimentos em IA, despencaram na quarta-feira (4), com investidores apostando que a onda democrata pode trazer licenciamentos mais rigorosos, custos mais elevados e um ritmo de construção mais lento.

"Precisamos fazer um trabalho melhor para comunicar de fato às pessoas, às comunidades locais, os motivos pelos quais os data centers podem ser benéficos para elas", afirmou o presidente da OpenAI, Greg Brockman, em um evento separado na quarta-feira (4).

Executivos da OpenAI, juntamente com muitos de seus pares do setor de tecnologia, passaram os últimos dois anos tentando estreitar laços com o governo do presidente Donald Trump na esperança de moldar as políticas federais voltadas à IA.

O presidente-executivo da OpenAI, Sam Altman, doou US$ 1 milhão (R$ 5,48 milhões) para o comitê de posse de Trump e posteriormente compareceu à sua segunda cerimônia de posse. No ano passado, Brockman doou US$ 25 milhões (R$ 136,67 milhões), em caráter pessoal, para um super PAC de apoio a Trump.

Questionado sobre como a empresa pretende enfrentar o ceticismo de parte dos democratas, Lehane destacou sua vasta experiência no partido. Durante o governo Clinton, ele atuou como advogado sênior e porta-voz, além de ter sido secretário de imprensa do vice-presidente Al Gore na campanha presidencial deste último.

Denoising refines noisy, compressed video representations into a segment. Temporal-causal attention lets it read available history and controls while blocking future target observations. A key/value cache retains attention information for the next prediction.

The model operates on compressed video representations called latents. After the initial image, each latent time step represents four RGB frames. Transfer predicts one step per chunk; the optimized Bimanual setup in the report predicts two. Smaller chunks let new controls affect generation more often.

- Stage 0 — Bidirectional adaptation: learn the domain and its control-to-video relationships from complete recorded clips.

- Stage 1 — Teacher forcing: predict each chunk from earlier recorded observations and available controls, with the clean answer and future targets masked out.

- Stage 2 — Consistency distillation: use a frozen teacher to train a student that generates each chunk in four denoising steps.

- Stage 3 — Self-forcing: train on the student’s generated history. A frozen teacher and an auxiliary score model supply the distribution-matching signal.

Why the final stage matters: a slightly mispredicted object becomes context for later frames, so errors can compound. Self-forcing exposes training to that generated history. Distillation and caching reduce the cost of producing each observation.

The post-training recipes start from Cosmos3-Nano or compatible base weights and cover all four stages. Keep full training checkpoints to resume; exported inference weights omit optimizer and other training state.
