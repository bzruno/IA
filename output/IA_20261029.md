## 1. Mistral releases weights for its trillion-parameter AI model, targeting cybersecurity

- **Fonte:** TechCrunch AI
- **Publicado em:** 2026-10-29 14:00
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 9.5
- **Link:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/29/mistral-releases-weights-for-its-trillion-parameter-ai-model-targeting-cybersecurity/

French AI company Mistral has released the weights for its trillion-parameter open source AI model Mistral Large 4 (ML4), allowing businesses and governments to download and deploy the system themselves. Nicknamed “Le Chonk,” the model was first introduced earlier this month.

The release comes as competition between open-weight AI developers like Mistral and closed-model providers like OpenAI and Anthropic heats up, with the former arguing that businesses and governments shouldn’t have to rely on outside AI companies for access to advanced capabilities. That argument may hold more weight in cybersecurity, where organizations want more control over their data and systems.

Or, as Mistral’s CEO, Arthur Mensch, explained, AI should “be a tool for empowerment, not dominance.” With Chonk, Mistral doubles down on that position, arguing that it’s able to achieve “state-of-the-art performance” at a “fraction of the compute and capital committed by closed labs.”

The release of the model’s weights — the numerical training that animates the system — ensures that other companies and government agencies will now be able to deploy ML4 for their own purposes.

Mistral says that its model is comparable in terms of performance with a Chinese open-weight competitor, GLM-5.3, which was previously considered one of the best open-weight models in the world — particularly when it comes to cyber work. However, Mistral’s model uses 47 percent fewer tokens per task, making it significantly more economical than GLM-5.3.

The company also claims that the model outranks all other open models when it comes to agentic coding tasks on Artificial Analysis’ Coding Agent Index, an AI benchmarking site.

“Amid growing cyber threats, ML4 brings state-of-the-art sovereign cyber defense capabilities to enterprises and governments globally,” the company said Thursday. “Enterprise and public sector institutions can now download, deploy, and own frontier AI to detect, analyze, and respond to threats across code and IT infrastructure.”

The weights for ML4 are being made available on Hugging Face and via its API.

Pierre Stock, Vice President of Science at Mistral, told TechCrunch that the release of the model’s weights meant that organizations will have more control over how they use AI to defend themselves.

“Concretely, it means that, for instance, as a business company or as a government, you’re able to have access to frontier intelligence to defend yourselves against cyberattacks. Access that can’t be cut off by definition because the model is open-weighted,” he said.

Stock added that the model could be used to “identify, coordinate, and respond in real time to major security incidents, and that the open sourcing means that companies can deploy this model on-prem in their own ecosystem.

While advances in AI cybersecurity are helping security professionals to find bugs at a much higher rate than before, Stock continued, he believes it’s important for companies to “provide [this same] capability to everyone…as soon as possible to ramp up and to strengthen the ecosystem,” given that “some malicious actors might try to actually leverage their own technology to attack.”

Separately, Stock addressed concerns about recent incidents involving rogue AI agents, acknowledging the risks but cautioning against overreacting.

“We don’t want to fearmonger people. We think it’s a problem that is solvable. It’s a hard problem to have good security,” he said.

## 7. Alphabet earnings miss reflects first real sign that AI is taking a bite out of search

- **Fonte:** CNBC Technology
- **Publicado em:** 2026-10-29 17:16
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 6.0
- **Link:** https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/29/alphabet-google-search-ai-earnings.html

In Alphabet's across-the-board earnings miss, one segment stood out above all else: search.

Revenue in the search and other segment grew 12% from a year ago to roughly $63 billion, but came in below estimates for the second straight quarter. At the same time, Google said search query growth on the platform was the strongest it's been in five years.

The gap between usage and revenue within the search giant's flagship business is becoming harder to dismiss as more people turn to artificial intelligence bots and agents, including tools offered by Google. Since the early days of ChatGPT, analysts have predicted that traditional search will get eclipsed by AI, and warned that Google will have to figure out new ways to make money. While search remains a massive business, early signs of a shift are appearing.

"These things are going to be a force," Deepwater Asset Management's Gene Munster told CNBC's "Fast Money" after the earnings report, referring to new AI-powered services. Munster said that if "personalized bots" take off, "that will become for a lot of people the starting point of commerce, which impacts where Google is."

Alphabet executives said foreign exchange was the biggest factor behind the deceleration in search revenue in the period, and said the currency impact would be even greater in the fourth quarter.

The stock plummeted about 13% on Thursday, the steepest drop on record, reflecting the weaker-than-expected earnings report and negative free cash flow of $9 billion for the quarter, three times the estimated deficit. In the second quarter, Alphabet's free cash flow went negative for the first time in its history as a public company, at $5.9 billion.

Google has spent much of the past year telling investors that it needs to pour money into AI infrastructure and services to meet unending demand from consumers and businesses. Google's Gemini model has allowed the company to stay competitive with Anthropic and OpenAI, and its Gemini-powered enterprise services have proven to be a hit with customers.

The company also says that Gemini is strengthening its core digital ads business, as it uses AI to improve its ability to understand user intent and make advertising more relevant.

“We continue to accelerate the deployment of Gemini models across our entire ads infrastructure to boost performance,” Chief Business Officer Philipp Schindler said on the third-quarter earnings call.

The company made what Schindler called its “biggest update to product listing ads in a decade” during the third quarter, adding new AI capabilities.

“Overall, AI is accelerating our ads development and drives the majority of our product launches,” Schindler said.

Advertisers are also rapidly adopting AI tools. In the third quarter, Gemini models helped advertisers generate more than 1.5 billion campaign assets, up more than sevenfold from a year earlier, Schindler said.

But none of that addresses revenue growth for search, which slid from 19% in the first quarter to 17% in the second, before falling to 12% in the third.

Analysts at UBS wrote in a note that the results are "going to bring Search cannibalization fears right back to the forefront." And Mizuho analyst Jordan Klein said the numbers are an issue for search and YouTube.

"This elevates the fear around AI and agentic taking share from Search and YT and hurting their AI monetization narrative," Klein wrote in a note after the earnings report.

Where Alphabet's AI bet is very much paying off is in its cloud business.

Revenue in that division, which is racing to catch up to Amazon Web Services and Microsoft Azure, more than doubled in the quarter to nearly $31 billion. Cloud backlog also reached roughly $560 billion, reflecting enormous contracted demand for AI infrastructure. Management said it expects just more than half of that backlog to be recognized over the next 24 months.

In Microsoft's quarterly earnings report, which also landed after the bell Wednesday, the software maker said Azure revenue increased 48% from a year earlier to $33.2 billion.

Cloud demand is a big reason for Alphabet to keep spending.

The company maintained its full-year 2026 capital expenditure forecast of $195 billion to $205 billion and said spending will increase significantly in 2027. Some of that is for its tensor processing units, or TPUs, which compete with Nvidia's graphics processing units. Alphabet just started selling them to outside customers in the second quarter.

Customer agreements first show up in Google Cloud’s backlog, and the company has to spend money building the systems before it can deliver them and recognize the associated revenue.

“You see cash going out and we’re building up the inventory base,” Alphabet CFO Anat Ashkenazi said on the earnings call. “As we start delivering and billing customers for these TPU systems, then you see cash coming in.”

Still, Google has to contend with concerns about its longtime cash cow. Gil Luria, an analyst at D.A. Davidson, said the fears that existed from 2023 to 2025 about the AI effect appear to finally be showing up in Google's business.

"Investors will now worry that the miss in Search, YouTube and Subscription, leading to a profit miss, are an indication that AI has finally come for Google," Luria wrote in an email. "We would not rush to conclusions based on one quarter, but will definitely need to reassess what the world of agentic commerce means for Google."

Andy Elder and Arjun Mathur were Stanford roommates who both studied computer science before spending a decade at Palantir. Elder led product design for a team of 800 people building software for U.S. defense and intelligence agencies, while Mathur served as a senior vice president in the company’s commercial practice. When the friends decided to build a startup early last year, there was no question they would do it together.

Despite having no direct background in architecture, Elder and Mathur were troubled by a growing problem: Fewer buildings are constructed in the U.S. today than a few decades ago, as development costs have surged.

“We’ve gone from dial-up internet to superintelligent AI, but our ability to build the physical world around us has actually decreased,” Mathur told TechCrunch.

Believing AI can boost productivity across the architecture, engineering, and construction (AEC) industry, they set out to build Dream, a startup developing built world models that automate building design and engineering. Dream has emerged from stealth with $17 million in seed funding led by Andreessen Horowitz, alongside other backers, including SV Angel, XYZ, and Zigg Capital. The startup aims to replace cumbersome, outdated AEC tools like Autodesk’s AutoCAD and Revit.

If you sit next to an architect or a structural engineer, you’ll notice that they are still “drawing” every piece of their designs part by part on legacy software, Mathur said. “AI really plays no role in that equation.”

Just as LLMs learn from text, Dream’s world model is trained on more than 100 million square feet of building data gathered from permit archives across the United States.

“Our model actually understands what’s within those walls. What materials are used, what the beams are made of, and how much weight they can support,” Elder said.

The startup calls its approach “structural cognition” because its model generates structurally precise designs that account for internal building elements like plumbing and HVAC.

Dream has already begun selling its product to AEC professionals and developers, who use the model to conceptualize building designs and estimate overall costs, according to the founders. The platform is already deployed across 10 active projects in Texas and California.

While Dream isn’t the only company attempting to update AEC software with AI — other startups working on this include Motif, Snaptrude, and Arcol — Elder and Mathur claim that they are the only ones that developed a world model that instantly designs whole buildings.

Kimberly Tan, a partner at Andreessen Horowitz, said that while the firm first invested in Dream over a year ago, when the startup model was just an idea, she has been extremely impressed with their progress. “I have not seen anybody take the technical approach,” she said. “They’re doing everything based on their own models.”

Following the Hugging Face breach, Mistral was one of many companies that signed Nvidia’s Open Secure AI Alliance, a cross-industry partnership that argues open models are crucial to democratizing defenses against increasingly common AI security incidents.

By Nvidia’s logic, and its Alliance that aims to democratize AI security tools, the race is between Mistral and other open models to achieve state-of-the-art security prowess.

A few weeks ago, French company Mistral released an early version of its new artificial intelligence model, nicknamed Le Chonk. At the time, the company said it was the most advanced open-weight offering outside of China. After further refinement, Mistral now says Le Chonk is the “world’s best” open-weight model for automated coding and cybersecurity.

The advances, Mistral says, were made possible by feeding the model increasingly difficult cybersecurity and coding problems, which allowed the trillion-parameter Le Chonk to match or surpass models from leading Chinese labs like DeepSeek and Z.ai.

“It’s a pivotal moment in the global AI leadership race,” Pierre Stock, VP of science at Mistral, told reporters during a press briefing. “It offers frontier capabilities for enterprises and governments to defend against an increasing threat level in cyber.”

The most high-profile episode occurred when one of OpenAI’s models broke loose from a testing sandbox and hacked into multiple companies over the summer. It sparked panic over the idea that AI could escape human oversight and be abused by threat actors to launch sophisticated cyberattacks. (One of the affected companies, Hugging Face, used an open-weight model from China to assess and defend against the attack.)

Since then, AI has unearthed security vulnerabilities in ubiquitous software, too. In October, a raft of websites were taken offline after an AI tool discovered serious flaws in a foundational piece of web technology.

Mistral pitches its open-weight models, like Le Chonk, as part of the solution. If AI is destined to become a central feature of both cyber offense and defense, the argument goes, businesses and governments will need to be equipped with models that are freely available and cannot be switched off remotely, unlike proprietary models from the likes of OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google.

“AI is going to be like electricity. You don’t want to live in a world in which your neighbor can cut off electricity from one day to another,” Stock said. “Once you open source the weights, they’re all over the internet. It’s impossible to cut off access.”

The trade-off, however, is that freely available models can also be downloaded and customized by threat actors.

“It’s clearly a dual-use technology,” Rafe Pilling, senior director of threat intelligence at security company Sophos, tells WIRED. “Anything that’s available to download and circulate unfettered has the ability to be abused as well as used for productive purposes.”

According to Pilling, though it’s possible a Chinese model with internet access could siphon away data, all advanced models are capable of going rogue without careful supervision—no matter their country of origin.

“The technical realities and the narrative—which often has a nationalistic leaning—are two different things,” he says.“It’s about having the right monitoring and controls in place to be confident that, even if the model was trying to be deceptive or nefarious, you might have some way of detecting it.”

## 15. As search traffic dries up, Reddit announces it’s shifting to a logged-in experience

- **Fonte:** TechCrunch AI
- **Publicado em:** 2026-10-29 17:08
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 5.0
- **Link:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/29/as-search-traffic-dries-up-reddit-announces-its-shifting-to-a-logged-in-experience/

Reddit has long attracted millions of users to its site through Google, with many even appending the word “Reddit” to their search queries for better answers. But as search traffic dries up amid the shift to AI-powered search, Reddit is rethinking its relationship with the open internet. In its third-quarter earnings call on Thursday, the company announced it’s transitioning to a primarily “logged-in experience.”

The move is designed to protect its data from AI scraping, allowing the company to instead forge lucrative licensing deals with model providers. But just as important, such a shift could help Reddit to increase its revenue even as it faces daily active user declines in its largest market, the U.S.

Overall, Reddit’s global daily active users were flat quarter-over-quarter in Q3, reaching 130.3 million, while U.S. daily active users dropped from 53.2 million in Q2 to 50.7 million in Q3 and were down 2% year-over-year.

The company is not alone in seeing such declines; the shift to an AI-centered web has impacted most websites and publishers by now, leading to fewer click-throughs, as AI summaries provide answers that don’t require website visits and Google’s traditional “ten blue links” have reduced relevance. (The change is now even impacting Google’s bottom line, its earnings revealed.)

But at Reddit’s scale, the numbers associated with such a decline are enormous. The company said search traffic referrals dropped by more than 1 million referrals on average per week in October. It warned that if the trend continues, daily active users could decline to a low-double-digit percentage by next quarter. (To play that out: a hypothetical 10% to 12% decline could bring Reddit to roughly 115 million to 117 million daily actives, down from 130.3 million in Q3. That’s a huge drop!)

At the same time, Reddit has gotten significantly better at monetizing its audience even as its growth weakens. Revenue increased 53% year-over-year to $895 million, ahead of Wall Street’s expectations of about $875 million. Net income rose 74% to $283 million. Advertising revenue grew 54% to $847 million while nonadvertising revenue, which includes its AI data-licensing business, grew 35% year-over-year to $48 million.

And despite a 2% year-over-year decline in U.S. daily users, Reddit’s average revenue per daily active user in the U.S. rose 53% to $13.82. Globally, average revenue per daily active user increased 36% year-over-year to $6.87.

Shifting to a logged-in experience could boost those numbers even higher because logged-in users are typically worth more to advertisers.

Unlike the logged-out users who often find their way to the site via search, logged-in users provide more signals about their interests and behaviors, which helps to better target and personalize ads.

Those same signals would also help Reddit provide a more personalized experience to its users, which includes personalized feeds, recommendations of new communities to subscribe to, and pushes to vote, comment, participate, and more. (This also includes pushing recommendations to its new algorithmic “For You” feed, announced yesterday, which will combine posts from communities users belong to alongside other recommendations based on their interests.)

Still, a change of this magnitude could be the canary in the coal mine for the open web.

“I don’t know about you, but as someone whose life has been defined by the open internet, it’s a big change for me,” wrote Reddit co-founder and CEO Steve Huffman in a post published on Reddit’s site. “But I’ve come to the conclusion that this is the right decision for our platform and the right decision for our business,” he said.

The company notes that the shift to the logged-in experience will not be an overnight change; it will first run tests in select markets outside the U.S. in the fourth quarter. It doesn’t yet have a timeline for when the change will be rolled out to all its users worldwide. It also acknowledged that access to a logged-out front page is important.

Plus, Reddit pointed out that being logged in doesn’t mean having to give up anonymity, nor will it remove its option to browse anonymously through its “incognito mode” experience, which is being rebuilt.

“There are still plenty of questions that are unanswered, particularly around where the boundaries should be between logged-in and logged-out experiences,” Hoffman wrote. “This is a big evolution for Reddit, and not one we’re taking lightly. But I believe it’s the right next step for the platform and for our future.”
