Anthropic is seeking a valuation of up to $1.4tn (£1.1tn) in its imminent stock market flotation, short of the $2tn that had been expected before investors started to question the durability of the AI boom.

The developer of the Claude chatbot was widely reported to be targeting a $2tn initial public offering on Wall Street but its sights have been set lower, according to a US regulatory filing on Tuesday.

Anthropic is aiming to raise up to $64.4bn by selling 825m shares in the IPO priced up to $78 a share. The California-based company will also raise a further $26bn via a separate private placement deal with cornerstone investors including the Google parent, Alphabet, and the chipmaker Nvidia.

An extra $9.7bn could be raised from a so-called greenshoe offering, which kicks in if investor demand is high and would take the total amount raised by Anthropic to approximately $100bn if the shares sold at the top end of the price range.

At the upper bracket of the $70-$78 per share price range, Anthropic would be worth $1.4tn on a fully diluted basis – which means taking into account all the common shares in the company in existence, including employee stock options.

Trading in Anthropic shares is expected to begin next week.

Investor jitters over the AI boom were crystallised last week when the US midterm elections produced victories for datacentre opponents. This has added to already existing concerns, such as how quickly – or if at all – companies can generate a return on their spending on AI infrastructure.

Brian Hopkins, a principal analyst at Forrester Research, said the midterm elections gave investors the chance to correct valuations that they were beginning to have concerns about, because of worries about the financial sustainability of the AI build-out. The election results brought those concerns to a head, Hopkins said.

“The market needed an excuse to correct itself a little bit in terms of valuations,” he said. “The US election spook is what has caused it.”

As a consequence, Anthropic’s valuation has also been lowered from an anticipated $2tn, he added.

The largest ever stock market listing is Elon Musk’s SpaceX, which joined the US stock market with a valuation of $1.78tn, raising $75bn. The company, whose assets include Musk’s rocket launch business and the X social media platform, is now worth just under $2tn.

However, the $100bn that could be raised in the Anthropic offering will exceed the amount raised by SpaceX.

Dario Amodei, Anthropic’s chief executive, is selling about $260m worth of shares in the float, as are his six fellow co-founders. At the top of the price range the co-founders’ stakes will be worth more than $18bn each.

A Chinese spying scandal enveloping Nvidia, a cornerstone company for AI investors, also threatens to overshadow the industry but has not affected share prices so far – other than Nvidia’s.

The updated Anthropic filing stated that the company’s annual revenue is now running at $81bn as of 31 October, up $10bn from the previous month.

Anthropic’s main expenditure is on the computing power, from datacentres, that is key to developing and operating its AI systems. According to its prospectus, Anthropic has compute purchase commitments and lease agreements of more than $70bn over the next 12 months and more than $450bn by 2030, with a further $450bn due by 2045.

In a podcast appearance earlier this year, Anthropic’s Amodei explained how compute commitments had to be matched by revenue growth.

“I could buy $1tn of compute that starts at the end of 2027. If my revenue is not $1tn, if it’s even $800bn, there’s no force on Earth, there’s no hedge on Earth, that could stop me from going bankrupt,” he told podcaster Dwarkesh Patel.

On Tuesday the Financial Times reported that the investment bank Goldman Sachs, which is leading the float along with Morgan Stanley, is forecasting Anthropic will generate annual revenues of $600bn by 2030.

## 4. Introducing AlphaProof Code: a breakthrough in verifying complex software at depth

- **Fonte:** Google DeepMind
- **Publicado em:** 2026-11-10 09:02
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 10.5
- **Link:** https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/

How Google DeepMind researchers built a new agentic framework that formally verified the code of Signal’s cryptographic protocol — long considered to be a grand security challenge

Today, we’re sharing a milestone in software security long considered to be a grand challenge: Google DeepMind researchers guided AI agents to mathematically prove key security properties of the actual code underlying the Signal protocol, the technology that protects billions of private conversations in apps like Signal and Google Messages.

This breakthrough discovery was made using AlphaProof Code, a novel agentic framework for formal verification at unprecedented depth that combines human expertise with AI agents. Formal verification—mathematically proving code runs correctly without flaws—is the highest standard of security assurance; however, it is difficult and often labor-intensive.

Our work marks the first time AI has been used to formally verify a widely deployed cryptographic protocol at this depth. It's also the first time the implementation of a widely deployed protocol like this has been formally verified by any method, with or without AI.

How we verified Signal

AlphaProof Code is a multi-LLM agentic framework that splits the work of formal verification the way a research team would: human experts work with AI to decide what to prove, and a group of AI agents does it.

First, our researchers collaborated with a mix of models to translate Signal’s security goals into formal mathematical specifications, with human experts reviewing every one. From there, agents powered by Gemini Carbon did the proofs, generating over six million lines of Lean, a computer language that mathematically proves the accuracy of code.

Next, the agents audited the proof and highlighted areas that needed a second look by a human – just one percent of the codebase, a massive time savings. Because Lean automatically checks the rest, our experts could focus their review where human judgment matters most.

Cryptographic protocols underpin nearly all digital communication, from online banking to web browsing, and a single flaw can expose millions of people at once. That's why experts have spent decades formally analyzing them.

But most protocol verification stops at the design level. Our agents went further, proving that the compiled code fulfills the key security properties – such as keeping messages protected before and after a device is temporarily compromised – down to the exact code running on devices. Because real-world vulnerabilities often hide in the gap between design and implementation, bridging this gap makes these proofs vastly more complex.

Even at this complexity, our verification process took just four weeks – a task that historically takes expert teams years – proving that Signal's core cryptographic code delivers the security protections our experts specified.

To confirm that AlphaProof Code can tell correct code from buggy code, we reintroduced two real, previously fixed bugs into an isolated version of Signal's code. In both cases, the system produced a machine-checked proof that the buggy version was insecure.

Kubernetes, the open source system that manages how applications run across thousands of AI factories, has become the foundation for AI, and the demands on it are only growing.

To help meet them, NVIDIA is deepening its commitment to open source at KubeCon + CloudNativeCon, the flagship conference for the Kubernetes and cloud-native communities.

NVIDIA is contributing NVIDIA OpenShell, an open source runtime for securing and governing autonomous agents, to the Cloud Native Computing Foundation (CNCF), a nonprofit that is part of the Linux Foundation and the host of KubeCon. As a CNCF Sandbox project, OpenShell will be governed by the organizations building it into their products and strengthened by the expertise of security professionals worldwide.

NVIDIA is also contributing to Agent Substrate, an open source Google-led framework for operating long-running agent workloads at scale on Kubernetes that has been accepted into the CNCF Sandbox.

Together, these advancements mark a step forward in making Kubernetes the leading foundation for AI workloads at scale — empowering developers to build safer, more efficient AI infrastructure for the future.

Securing AI Agents in the Open

NVIDIA’s contribution of OpenShell to CNCF puts security infrastructure for autonomous agents in the community’s hands to build, inspect and improve — accelerating development and hardening its safeguards.

“The cloud-native community has a new infrastructure challenge: giving agents secure environments in which to operate,” said Jim Zemlin, CEO of the Linux Foundation. “NVIDIA’s contribution of OpenShell to CNCF gives developers a shared foundation to build on and an opportunity to shape how agent security evolves. OpenShell gives organizations control over what AI agents can do. It applies deterministic controls to agent activity, so teams can enforce policies as agents use tools, access inference services and execute workloads. Its extensible architecture lets teams connect their own compute, inference providers, credential storage and monitoring tools. This allows organizations to apply governance across different deployment environments while integrating OpenShell with their existing enterprise systems.

Hundreds of organizations have put OpenShell to use, and the project has received tens of thousands of contributions in less than a year since launch.

Scaling Agent Execution on Kubernetes

Governing agents is only part of the challenge. Organizations also need to deploy them at scale.

NVIDIA is contributing to Agent Substrate, a Google-originated project in the CNCF Sandbox, and ensuring it works with NVIDIA Dynamo for model inference and KAI Scheduler for shared cluster resource allocation. This effort brings together agent execution, inference serving and resource scheduling on Kubernetes.

Agent Substrate preserves agent environments between tasks while releasing compute when agents are idle. By suspending and resuming those environments across a shared pool of agents, it lets teams scale agent workloads without keeping dedicated compute running for every session.

NVIDIA and Google are also collaborating on an integration of OpenShell and Agent Substrate, so enterprises can secure and govern agents across clouds and on-premises systems.

Building an Open Foundation for AI Factories

NVKE and NVKR are launching with ecosystem support that extends their reach across AI infrastructure platforms and cloud services. Mirantis, Netris, OpenNebula Systems, Rafay, Spectro Cloud and vCluster, together with Firmus and SK Telecom, have aligned with NVIDIA on integration plans to bring NVKE and NVKR capabilities into their offerings.

NVIDIA Snapshot addresses the challenge of bringing additional inference capacity online quickly. Loading models and warming up workloads can delay scaling and recovery. The open source, modular component restores checkpointed replicas in seconds, without model reload or warmup, supporting faster startup, recovery and elasticity for inference and agentic workloads on Kubernetes.

Design partners Crusoe and Saturn Cloud have evaluated Snapshot on their own workloads using NVIDIA HGX B300 systems. Crusoe, an upstream contributor to Snapshot, tested GLM-5.3 on Crusoe Cloud and reduced the time for the workload to become ready from roughly eight minutes to 31 seconds. Saturn Cloud, a token factory platform, benchmarked Snapshot for scale-from-zero inference and reduced vLLM startup times from between two and 8.5 minutes to six and 34 seconds.
