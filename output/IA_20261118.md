## 2. ついに“AIっぽさ”を脱却か？ OpenAIが新モデルGPT-6.2 AstraとSolを発表

- **Fonte:** ITmedia AI+
- **Publicado em:** 2026-11-18 19:00
- **Idioma:** ja
- **Cobertura:** 2 veículos noticiaram o caso

OpenAIの新AIモデル「GPT-6.2 Astra」と「GPT-6.2 Sol」が登場した。法務や財務などの高度な専門業務を担うAstraと、日々の業務を支えるSolは、従来のコスト常識を覆す高い効率性を備える。

OpenAIは、複雑な専門業務向けのAIモデル「GPT-6.2 Astra」と、日常業務向けの「GPT-6.2 Sol」を発表した。両モデルとも少ない出力トークンで高度な処理を実行できるのが特徴だ。

GPT-6.2 Astraで、ついに「AIっぽい」文章から脱却か

上位モデルのGPT-6.2 Astraは、法務や財務、会計、営業といった専門分野の業務を想定して開発された。元データから必要な数値を抽出して財務モデルや投資メモを作成する。ファイルやスクリーンショットからレイアウトや配色、フォントサイズなどを読み取って指定のテンプレートに沿ったスライドを作成する他、表計算シートや文書も各分野の慣行やテンプレートに沿って出力する。

同社によると、冗長な表現や定型的なまとめを減らして「AIっぽさ」を抑えた直接的で具体的な文章を作成できるという。

複数段階の専門業務を評価するベンチマーク「Agents' Last Exam」では、前世代の「GPT-6 Astra」を5.2ポイント上回り、3分の1未満のコストで最高スコアを記録した。OpenAIは「Claude Fable 5.2」に対して「GPT-6.2 Astraがわずか5％のコストで5.5ポイント上回った」としている。

コンサルティングや金融などの長期プロジェクトを模したOpenAIの社内ベンチマーク「GDPval v2」でも、GPT-6 Astraから出力トークンを22％削減しつつスコアを6.2ポイント伸ばした。

先行導入した企業は、トークン消費量と手戻りの削減を報告している。法務AIを手掛けるHarveyの応用研究責任者であるニコ・グルペン（Niko Grupen）氏は、法務エージェントの評価で出力トークンを34.5％減らしながら、GPT-6 Astraの3倍以上のタスクを完了したと説明する。ClickUpのAI責任者ジェイ・ハック（Jay Hack）氏は、アプリやスライド作成のブラインドテストでGPT-6.2 Astraが既存の採用モデルを上回ったと評価する。消費トークンを約40％、修正の手間を30％削減できたという。

コンピュータ操作やWebデザイン、コード生成の精度も向上した。CADやシミュレーションツールを部品単位で操作して設計変更に伴う負荷の移動を可視化できる他、テキストの指示から3Dのブラウザ体験やWebサイトのフロントエンドを構築できる。既存コードベースへの複雑な変更やテスト実装も効率化して、サイバーセキュリティ分野では脆弱（ぜいじゃく）性の発見や安全なコードの記述、パッチ作成の能力を高めた。

悪用リスクを抑えるため安全対策を強化しており、正当な脆弱性調査の一部も制限されるため、防御担当者向けには専用のアクセスプログラム「OpenAI Daybreak」への統合を進める。

同時発表されたGPT-6.2 Solは、日常業務や複数ステップのエージェントワークフローに向けた汎用（はんよう）モデルだ。コード変更の品質を測るCognitionのベンチマーク「FrontierCode 2.0」では、GPT-6.2 Astraの約5分の1のコストでスコアの差を0.5ポイント以内に抑えた。

引っ越し後の幼稚園選びや予算と通勤条件に合う住宅探しなど、日常の課題を測る「LifeVal」では、GPT-6 Astraの約7分の1のコストでスコアを上回ったという。

APIの100万トークン当たりの価格は、GPT-6.2 Astraが入出力共に前世代比20％減、キャッシュ済み入力単価は60％減となり、入力8ドル、キャッシュ済み入力0.40ドル、出力40ドルに設定された。GPT-6.2 Solは前世代の「GPT-6.1 Sol」の価格を据え置き、入力2ドル、キャッシュ済み入力0.10ドル、出力10ドルとなる。同社はキャッシュの再利用効率を高めてコストと遅延を抑えるため、プロンプトの先頭部分を変更せず新しい指示を末尾に追加する使い方を推奨している。

両モデルは「ChatGPT Work」および「Codex」向けに提供を開始した。対象は個人向けの「ChatGPT Plus」「ChatGPT Pro」に加え、法人・教育機関向けの「ChatGPT Enterprise」「ChatGPT Edu」の各プランとなっており、EnterpriseおよびEduではワークスペースの管理者が有効化することで利用できる。

高速処理機能の「Ultrafast」は、「ChatGPT Pro 500」契約者が両モデルで利用できる他、新たに「ChatGPT Pro 200」契約者にもGPT-6.2 Solから提供が始まった。開発者向けにはOpenAI APIを通じて「gpt-6.2-astra」および「gpt-6.2-sol」として提供しており、いずれもUltrafastに対応する。

## 3. Google、わずか2カ月で4つ目のモデル公開「Gemini 4 Neon」 一部「Fable同等」性能をコスト半分で

Google、わずか2カ月で4つ目のモデル公開「Gemini 4 Neon」 一部「Fable同等」性能をコスト半分で（1/2 ページ）

米Googleは11月18日（現地時間）、AIモデル「Gemini 4 Neon」（以下、Neon）を公開した。「Gemini 4」シリーズのモデルは「Gemini 4 Argon」（以下、Argon）、「Gemini 4 Carbon」（以下、Carbon）、「Gemini 4 Hydrogen」（以下、Hydrogen）に続き、2カ月足らずで4つ目となる。

Neonは上位モデルのCarbonを基にしたモデル。難度の高い長期プロジェクト向けのCarbonと、高速な日常作業向けのHydrogenの中間に当たり、推論の深さと効率のバランスを取ったという。Googleは開発者向けドキュメントで、Argonを一般的なコーディングや推論に使っている場合の移行先にNeonを挙げている。

モデルの性能評価を手掛ける米Artificial Analysisの「Coding Agent Index」では、Googleの開発者向けツール「Google Antigravity」（以下、Antigravity）のCLI版で動かしたNeon（思考量設定「xhigh」）が59ポイントを記録。米Anthropicの「Claude Code」で動かした「Claude Fable 5.2」（以下、Fable 5.2）（xhigh）の58ポイントと同水準だった。タスク当たりの平均コストは6.86ドルで、Fable 5.2（15.47ドル）の半分以下に収まった。

Artificial Analysisの総合指標「Intelligence Index」では、Neon（xhigh）が61ポイントでCarbon（high）を1ポイント上回り、Fable 5.2（max）、米OpenAIの「GPT-6.2 Astra」（max）に次ぐ3位だった。タスク当たりのコストは6.44ドルで、割り引き前の価格のCarbon（7.76ドル）より約17％安い。所要時間も約21分と、Carbon（約36分）より約40％短かった。

Googleは、Anthropicの「Claude Opus 5.5」（以下、Opus 5.5）とOpenAIの「GPT-6.2 Sol」を比較対象に、19項目の評価結果を公開。Neonは16項目で首位だった一方、ターミナル操作を伴うコーディング能力を測る「Terminal-Bench 4.1」は65.2％でOpus 5.5（65.8％）を下回るなど、3項目で他社モデルに届かなかった。

Neonは発表同日から、有料プラン「Google AI」の「Pro」「Ultra」加入者向けに「Gemini」アプリやAntigravityで順次提供している。GeminiアプリでCarbonを使えるのはUltra加入者に限られるが、NeonはProでも利用できる。企業向けには「Gemini for business」で提供する。

APIは有料枠のみで、料金は100万トークン当たり入力3ドル、キャッシュ済み入力0.15ドル、出力15ドル。Carbonの標準価格（入力4ドル、出力20ドル）より25％安いが、Carbonは11月30日まで半額の導入価格で提供しており、期間中はNeonの方が高い。

## 8. Simon Jack: Can Anthropic's record-breaking IPO stop the AI bubble bursting?

- **Fonte:** BBC Technology
- **Publicado em:** 2026-11-18 18:28
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 7.5

It was during November 2020 that somewhere in San Francisco, a woman called Daniela Amodei phoned a friend, asking for advice.

Daniela oversaw safety and policy at OpenAI, one of the most talked-about AI companies in the world.

But Daniela and her brother Dario - who worked there too - had misgivings over safety levels at OpenAI and thought it could be time to walk away.

So she did what many do when we can't make a decision about something, she phoned a friend. The friend's reply was direct and to the point: "You don't need to be on the phone with me. You already know the answer."

The Amodei siblings and a handful of other colleagues quit. Together, they set up a rival – Anthropic.

Six years on and Anthropic, the maker of leading AI model Claude, has predicted revenues of $52bn in 2026 and $135bn in 2027, according to analyst estimates.

It is selling shares to the public for the first time in what's known as an Initial Public Offering or IPO, aiming for a valuation of an estimated $1.4tn (£1tn), which would propel it straight into the top 10 of the most valuable US companies.

The sale comes at a crucial time when many are wondering whether, amid increasing concern about the potential of AI, the industry's bubble is showing signs of bursting.

Where once investors were "all in" on a technological revolution to rival the railroads, the car, electricity and the internet, some are now hedging their bets, amid arguments that AI prices will be driven down as the technology becomes more common and concerns that a hefty wedge of the AI investment is tech giants investing in each other.

Shares in companies that supply the raw computing power behind AI, like Coreweave and Nebius, have seen their value cut in half while even the mighty Nvidia has lost its crown as the world's most valuable company with its shares down 30% - a staggering $2tn fall in value.

So could the Anthropic IPO reinflate the bubble or will it be remembered as the last gasp of a boom that is heading for a bust?

A benchmark valuation

At an event last week, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei told JPMorgan Chase's CEO Jamie Dimon that his guiding principle was to "never let your company be worth a single dollar more than it's worth".

The company is selling roughly $81bn worth of shares to the general public and a further $26bn on to other big tech companies. It is comfortably the single biggest money raising event in US corporate history.

It's an old adage that during a gold rush you should buy companies that sell the picks and shovels as opposed to those staking everything on striking gold. In AI's case that's the chip makers, the data centre builders, the energy providers. But Anthropic is arguably the gold itself and this IPO will see what value people put on it, according to Fiona Cincotta from StoneX trading.

"I think the reason that this IPO is so important is basically it's the first ability for the public investors to access a pure play AI trade," she says, adding that previous offerings have been about "other parts of the ecosystem, which are, the semiconductors, the chips, the, data centres. But this is the first time that we were actually able to access the AI provider."

What a company is worth depends almost entirely on predicted future earnings.

It's that calculation that many think presents the sharpest needle to the AI bubble.

Will current and future users of AI be prepared to pay enough to justify the trillions being spent on the chips, data centres, energy and employees, some of whom can command annual salaries of over $100m?

"AI is certainly in a financial bubble. While LLMs [large language models] offer genuine utility and are undoubtedly here to stay, the economics don't add up," said Gary Marcus, Professor Emeritus at New York University and author of Taming Silicon Valley, speaking earlier this year.

"[The] more companies build AI models, the [more the] technology becomes commoditised, eliminating meaningful competitive advantages and driving down prices."

So far, the growth rates in Anthropic revenue are extraordinary.

But these explosive growth rates hide a potential problem. Around a quarter of that revenue came from just a handful of companies, among them Google and Amazon.

It's also concentrated in the US which accounts for two-thirds of its revenue.

There is also the staggering network of circular financing found in the AI world. For example, Anthropic has agreements to buy or lease hundreds of billions of dollars worth of chips and computing power from a handful of companies including Broadcom, Google, Amazon. Many of those firms are also investors in Anthropic and major customers of its products.

Public doubts

It's not just some Wall Street investors questioning whether the AI boom is all it's cracked up to be. Out on Main Street, ordinary Americans have concerns too.

With the US driving so much of global AI growth, a growing public backlash against a technology that threatens to replace millions of jobs and hoover up vast amounts of energy and water became a factor in the recent midterm elections in the US.

Many areas in the US have passed moratoriums on data centre development and many candidates deemed too close to big tech found that association a political liability rather than an asset. Many Democrat candidates publicly opposed data centre development and won.

Added to that, the Pied Piper of AI, chip maker Nvidia's CEO Jensen Huang, resigned last week after admitting to having been "unfaithful" to his wife after reports he had an affair with a suspected Chinese spy, Alice Huang and another with a company executive who reported to him.

Huang's spectacular fall from grace and Nvidia's 30% fall in value from its peak have left AI investors looking for a new champion for the AI boom that has underpinned the US stock market and arguably much of its entire economy for the last two years.

If Anthropic and its CEO are to be AI's new Pied Piper, he will be playing a different tune to the relentlessly upbeat, leather jacket-clad talisman Huang.

While Huang wanted AI development to continue full throttle, Anthropic was built on misgivings over the levels of safety and responsibility the OpenAI leavers felt were being exercised over the development of society-changing technology.

Amodei has warned that AI capabilities could enable biological weapon development, mass surveillance, authoritarianism and the replacement of millions of white-collar, tax-paying workers.

He has called for a slowdown in its development as its powers are growing at such an exponential rate that it could very soon elude any human oversight.

That said, his paternalistic overtures have been sceptically received by those who point out that the pace of Anthropic's development of new more powerful models has shown no sign of the slowdown he's advocating for the rest of the industry.

Hype and FOMO

But for all the concerns, just as with Elon Musk's SpaceX before it, the hype and the FOMO (fear of missing out) on an undoubtedly profound moment in technological and perhaps human development, means that buying interest is high.

It's reported that orders to buy shares exceed the number on offer by over six times.

With demand outstripping supply by such a margin, a first-day jump is to be expected given the oversubscription, but it doesn't tell you much about long-run return.

Using SpaceX as an example again, shares initially rose nearly 70% from its IPO sale price of $135 to $225. Shares in SpaceX are currently back to $145.

The Anthropic IPO could be wildly successful and blow a bit of fresh new air into what some maintain still looks a lot like a bubble.

Eileen Burbidge, founder of tech investors Passion Capital, is one of those who reject the notion that AI is a bubble about to burst.

"Maybe the growth of the bubble has slowed down a little bit, but actually I wouldn't be willing to say that it's about to burst yet," says Burbidge. "I think this IPO could actually mean everyone could be off to the races again."

Fiona Cincotta believes Anthropic's IPO is about far more than just Anthropic. "I think what's going to be really interesting here is I think it can actually be used as a valuation benchmark for the entire AI ecosystem," she says.

"That's why I don't think this is just going to affect the share price of Anthropic. I think this is going to have much wider implications across the whole AI trade."

Two things can be true at the same time. The Anthropic IPO could be wildly successful - FOMO is a powerful emotion in financial markets. But its probable success won't soothe the unease in some quarters that not every company, every investor and every citizen will think of AI as the answer to humanity's problems.
