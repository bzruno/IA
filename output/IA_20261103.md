- **Fonte:** CNBC Technology

Binance said hackers used fake job interviews, malicious software and compromised internal AI tools to infiltrate its wallet infrastructure, leading to the record-breaking Oct. 29 cyberattack that resulted in more than $6 billion in assets being stolen from the world's largest crypto exchange.

The attackers "spent months building their way in," Binance co-CEO Richard Teng told CNBC's "Squawk on the Street" Tuesday, rejecting suggestions that there was a single point of failure through one employee's laptop.

"In June, [the attackers] set up a fake version of a developer tool and lured one of our engineers to install that," he added. "In September they posed as recruiters and ran a series of job interviews with another engineer, and that ended up in a coding exercise that contained hidden malware designed to evade security ... from there they moved violently through our internal AI tools and obtain[ed] access that breach[ed] the system that authorized our wallet transaction."

He also said Binance has replenished 98% of $6.34 billion in affected assets, and that it is still in the process of purchasing outstanding, more illiquid assets.

Teng himself did not attribute the attack to North Korea but acknowledged that the FBI has linked it to North Korean hackers tied to the TraderTraitor group.

June preparations

Preparations for the attack began in June, when a Binance Wallet DevOps engineer downloaded a tampered Homebrew installation "to install a popular AI coding tool after searching for it on a search engine," according to a security report published by Binance Tuesday. It said it detected the suspicious activity, replaced the engineer's device and rotated credentials as a precaution, but that by then the employee's "role and his related colleagues on our wallet infrastructure team was exposed to the hacker."

As Binance works to recover from the incident, it has temporarily restricted certain withdrawals, pledged to cover all customer losses and tapped its own reserves to replenish affected assets. Binance and industry partners including Tether, Circle and Ripple have reportedly frozen about $3 billion in assets, though some stolen funds continue to move through laundered channels. Coinbase blocked transfers to compromised wallets and offered Binance direct assistance.

Although the incident represents a significant security breach, Binance maintains that it remains financially sound and focused on identifying the vulnerability, recovering remaining funds and preventing another attack.

The hack sparked a sharp but short-lived hit to confidence across crypto markets, especially compared with previous failures at major exchanges. Investors are now watching whether Binance can fully restore confidence in centralized exchanges.

As of Tuesday Binance has rebuilt the affected wallet system and internal AI tool, replaced compromised devices, decommissioned credentials and tokens associated with the affected engineers and expanded security scans across employee devices, Teng told "Squawk on the Street."

"We're also getting an independent expert to review everything that we have done, and we'll keep strengthening our defenses as the investigation continues," Teng said. "We're working around the clock with law enforcement agencies and with industry partners to trace and freeze the stolen funds for that front."

"Having the ability to control and turn off, and make a decision and decide, I think that's really important for this next chapter of AI adoption," Chief Business Officer Daniel Bernard told CNBC.

"If you kill a process at the root, and we have that level of access at the root … it's very binary," Bernard said. "You either kill the AI and the agent, or you don't, and then you report on that."

## 6. AI Is Changing How Semiconductor Fabs Work — and Engineers Are Moving Faster Because of It

- **Fonte:** Nvidia
- **Publicado em:** 2026-11-03 13:00
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 6.0
- **Link:** https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/ai-semiconductor-fabs/

Demand for advanced chips is rising. The processes to build them are getting more complex. And the margin for error is shrinking.

For engineers, understanding how a process change will behave, how a material will interact with a new step, how a defect maps back to a root cause are critical, challenging steps in semiconductor manufacturing — and these analyses can take days. Physical experiments on a fab line are slow and expensive. A wrong call on yield or process quality has real consequences.

AI is transforming semiconductor manufacturing, impacting what engineers can do — and how fast.

Accelerated computing shortens simulation time, allowing engineers to evaluate more alternatives before committing to physical trials.

Physics-based AI helps engineers predict process behavior without running a full simulation every time.

AI agents handle sequences of engineering tasks that once consumed hours.

And digital twins let teams test changes in a virtual environment before committing to physical trials.

Together, these capabilities describe an AI-defined fab: a semiconductor manufacturing factory where AI helps engineers optimize processes, catch problems earlier and make better decisions across the full manufacturing chip lifecycle. Engineers direct the work and make decisions based on their expertise while spending less time waiting for answers.

Shorter Simulation Cycles Change What Engineers Can Explore

Accelerated computing doesn’t just speed up individual calculations — it changes how many alternatives engineers can test before committing.

Multiphysics simulations model interactions among physical effects, such as heat transfer, electrical behavior and mechanical stress.

Using the NVIDIA cuDSS library, multiphysics software provider COMSOL is accelerating such simulations for semiconductor manufacturing by up to 10x. This helps engineers optimize equipment and processes, compare alternatives and decide which changes to test physically.

Applied Materials and NVIDIA are collaborating to accelerate atomic-scale materials discovery and process development. By integrating NVIDIA cuDSS, Applied’s Ginestra simulation platform runs up to 10x faster than CPU-only approaches. And with NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo, Applied’s ACE+ multiphysics platform delivers topography simulations up to 35x faster.

In some cases, acceleration doesn’t just shorten the calculation — it enables it altogether. Computational lithography — which defines how circuit patterns are printed onto silicon — involves calculations too intensive to complete on CPU at production scale. Siemens is integrating NVIDIA cuLitho into its Calibre lithography software, delivering up to 17x acceleration on key simulation workloads. That expands what engineers can verify before a wafer goes to print.

As such, faster calculations can help researchers explore material behavior and reaction pathways that influence next-generation semiconductor processes.

Predicting Process Behavior Without Running Every Simulation

In addition to accelerating simulations, AI trained on physics can predict how materials and processes will behave — without running a full simulation each time.

The payoff is most visible in complex calculations that are expensive even on accelerated hardware.

Plasma — the electrically charged gas used in semiconductor etch processes — is one example. Modeling plasma behavior requires solving equations that capture how electrical, chemical and thermal processes interact, making repeated simulation costly. Lam Research demonstrated that the NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo framework can predict plasma behavior in milliseconds, compared with days for a full simulation — giving engineers a faster way to explore process conditions without rerunning the full model each time.

Agents Handle the Steps Between Decisions

Engineers turn analyses into decisions — revising a design, investigating a test failure, adjusting a production process and more.

AI agents can help carry out the steps between those decisions.

Evaluating which AI model to apply to a given task is one example: a sequential, manual process that can consume hours on the test floor.

Advantest is using NVIDIA NeMo Switchyard AI agents to automate that selection, reducing response times from hours to minutes.

Testing Factory Changes Before They Reach the Line

Digital twins let teams simulate equipment configurations, layouts and process changes in a virtual environment before committing to physical trials. This reduces experimentation costs, especially as fabs become more complex and the stakes of a wrong change get higher.

Applied Materials uses NVIDIA Omniverse libraries to build physically accurate digital twins of entire fabs. Teams can simulate material movement, identify bottlenecks and evaluate layouts and operating strategies — extending virtual experimentation beyond individual processes to how the factory works as a whole.

The Connected Loop

The future of semiconductor manufacturing lies in connecting these capabilities: using production data to inform models and models to evaluate changes, then bringing those findings back to the engineers making production decisions.

That’s the promise of the AI-defined fab: where AI helps people learn from each production cycle and act on that knowledge sooner.

## 8. ‘A serious failure’: CPS prosecutor cites imaginary case to court after AI search

- **Fonte:** The Guardian (IA)
- **Publicado em:** 2026-11-03 03:00
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 6.0
- **Link:** https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/03/a-serious-failure-cps-prosecutor-cites-imaginary-case-to-high-court-after-ai-search

A senior crown prosecutor acting cited an imaginary legal precedent in an error that has sparked fresh concerns about AI hallucinations infecting the justice system.

Ravinder Dhillon, representing the Crown Prosecution Service (CPS), referenced an entirely made-up case during a dispute over whether a child should be prosecuted for the rape of his younger sister, high court documents have revealed.

Her explanation for how the nonexistent case came to be relied on has been called “unsatisfactory” by two senior high court judges and a “serious failure” by an expert in AI and law. The Guardian understands the reference came from an AI response generated during an internet search.

The case adds to dozens of others where AI-related errors have plagued court proceedings this year, mostly from people who are not legally qualified representing themselves, and often in employment tribunal cases.

It is only the second known case involving the CPS – the main public agency bringing criminal prosecutions in England and Wales – which admitted in February that one of its lawyers acting in an extradition case involving two Romanians convicted of minor offences cited nonexistent legal authorities, likely caused by AI. Those made-up cases were only spotted when opposition lawyers could find no trace of them and the CPS concluded “the risk of recurrence was low”.

In the latest case, lawyers for the boy, who was 11 at the time of the alleged rape of his seven-year-old sister, were challenging the CPS’s decision to prosecute him. They alleged prosecutors had failed to take into account representations from social workers, youth workers and a forensic psychologist. Dhillon, a senior crown prosecutor in the CPS’s rape and serious sexual offences unit, was arguing in the youth court in favour of the prosecution of the child and submitted a detailed reference to a case she called CPS – R v J [2024] EWCA Crim 3529. It turned out not to exist.

The decision to proceed was taken by Dhillon in April and confirmed by the chief crown prosecutor. Then in October Dhillon admitted that the nonexistent citation had been made in error. She said she had been referring to a completely different decision – this time a real one – that was made 15 years earlier.

“This was used as a legal source to argue for the prosecution of a child and that legal source doesn’t exist: that’s a serious failure,” said Brian Flanagan, associate professor of law at Maynooth University in Ireland who investigates the use of AI by judges. “It calls into question norms of trust in the law where we treat authorities as precious things. You can’t make them up; it’s out of bounds. AI increases the risk that is going to happen because of the time you can save. It is not great for any professional to do this, but when the professional is working for the state it takes on a different aspect, because the power of the state is immense.”

The high court said the CPS’s reference to a fictitious case did not affect the outcome of the boy’s challenge to the decision to continue with his prosecution, which is listed to go to trial in the youth courts later this week.

In June 2025, the high court responded to AI hallucinations in cases spanning housing to banking, by calling on the Bar Council and the Law Society to consider steps to curb the problem “as a matter of urgency”. Dame Victoria Sharp, then president of the King’s bench division, warned of “serious implications for the administration of justice and public confidence in the justice system if artificial intelligence is misused” and said lawyers misusing AI could face sanctions, from public admonishment to contempt of court proceedings and referral to the police.

Asked for comment, the CPS said it has a strict AI policy “which limits its use so that it is not used to make legal decisions and there must be human checks for accuracy”.

A spokesperson said the CPS “identified an error had occurred from conducting legal research using the internet”.

“As soon as this error was identified, it was remedied and an internal review was carried out to establish how this had occurred and to check that no other cases have been affected,” they said. “Following the rapid review we shared learnings, including that case law must be cited from trusted legal sources.”

It is understood the internet search delivered an AI summary which contained the fictional case details.

The latest incident brings to 101 the number of entries in a UK tracker maintained by Doughty Street Chambers barrister Matthew Lee covering confirmed, suspected and disputed AI-related errors, as well as related false citations. He said the cases identified may be the “tip of the iceberg”, as many lower court decisions are never published.

He added there are privacy and confidentiality risks associated with the CPS using AI, given the highly sensitive information it handles, and warned that AI was creating additional work for barristers, who need to spend time checking the authenticity of legal authorities appearing in case papers where AI may have been used in their preparation.

## 10. Anthropic、AIの応答が「本当にそのモデルによるものか」を暗号技術で証明する「FlashProve」を公開

- **Relevância:** 5.0
- **Link:** https://www.itmedia.co.jp/aiplus/article/2611/04/2000002863/

米Anthropicは11月2日（現地時間）、AIモデルの応答が、実際に指定したモデルによって生成されたものであることを暗号技術で証明するシステム「FlashProve」を発表した。コードや技術文書をApache 2.0ライセンスでGitHubに公開した。同社のFrontier Red Teamが、スイス連邦工科大学ローザンヌ校（EPFL）や米マサチューセッツ工科大学（MIT）の研究者らと共同で開発した。同社は、完成した成果ではなく、研究の途中経過の共有だとしている。

現状では、ユーザーはAIの応答が実際にどのモデルから返ってきたのかを確かめられない。Anthropicは例として、ルーティングサービスがコスト削減のためにユーザーが選んだモデルをひそかに別のモデルに差し替えるケースや、攻撃者が応答を改ざんするケースを挙げている。こうした場合、出力の品質だけでは見分けられないという。多くのモデルは重み（パラメータ）が非公開で、仮に入手できても、同じモデルを動かして検証するには、一般のユーザーや評価機関の手に余る計算資源が必要になる。

FlashProveは、計算が正しく実行されたことを中身を明かさずに短い証明で示せる「zkSNARK」と呼ばれる暗号技術を使う。ブロックチェーン分野などで実用されている技術だ。モデル提供者は事前にモデルの重みに対応する短い“指紋”（コミットメント）を公開し、以降は応答ごとに「この重みのモデルが、このプロンプトからこの出力を生成した」ことを示す証明ファイルを作成する。受け取った側はGPUのないPCでも証明を確認でき、出力が別のモデルによるものであれば検証に失敗する。証明から重みの情報は漏れないが、モデルのアーキテクチャは秘匿の対象外としている。

Anthropicは用途として、第三者の評価機関が評価対象と実際に提供されているモデルの同一性を確かめることや、推論サーバが乗っ取られた際の改ざん検知、承認済みモデルだけの提供を求める規制への準拠確認などを挙げている。同社は安全性に関する計画「Frontier Safety Roadmap」で、AIの出力を特定のモデルの重みにひも付けて検証する技術のプロトタイプを9月30日までに開発する目標を掲げていた。この目標は推論トラフィックに署名し、信頼できるサーバで再計算する別のプロジェクトで達成済みだとしており、FlashProveは同じ目的を別の手法で追求するものだという。

検証には米Metaの「Llama 4 Maverick」（4000億パラメータのMixture of Experts（MoE）モデル、4ビット量子化版）を使った。米NVIDIAのGPU「H100」1基で毎秒約110トークンの速度で証明を生成でき、1024トークンのリクエスト当たりの証明サイズは約19MB、24コアのCPUでの検証時間は約17秒だった。通常の推論の約70～95倍のGPU時間を要するが、同じモデルを対象とした既存のオープンソースの取り組みでは、1000トークン分の証明に14.3時間、証明サイズは93.6GBだったという。

Anthropicによると、従来のシステムに対する性能改善はClaudeによるもので、実行コードの多くもAIが生成した。一方、AIが構築した大規模で複雑なシステムは判断の妥当性を評価しにくいことから、セキュリティ上の主要な性質を形式検証言語「Lean」で数学的に証明し、第三者が監査できる形で公開した。

現時点では研究用のプロトタイプで、対象はMoEモデルに限られ、扱えるコンテキストは1024トークンまでと短い。証明の対象は整数化したモデルで、常に最も確率の高いトークンを選ぶ「greedy decoding」方式（貪欲法）を前提とする。また、証明は応答を返した後に生成するため、改ざんを未然に防ぐのではなく事後に検知する仕組みだ。

## 13. Agentic cheminformatics with Google Antigravity

- **Fonte:** Google DeepMind
- **Publicado em:** 2026-11-03 14:01
- **Idioma:** en
- **Relevância:** 5.0
- **Link:** https://deepmind.google/blog/agentic-cheminformatics-with-google-antigravity/

Chemistry is one of science’s richest public records — spanning millions of structures across journal articles and vendor catalogs. It provides a common representation that links physics and mathematics to the life and environmental sciences. Molecules and their transformations are ubiquitous and shape the everyday world.

Despite decades of progress in cheminformatics, chemists still spend too much time wrangling data, a process complicated by the fact that molecules can be represented in many ways: from text descriptions to images or data structures. The same molecule can appear as an IUPAC name in a paper, a Markush structure in a patent, or a structure-data file (SDF) in a catalog. Advanced cheminformatics systems must be able to unify these sources and deal with the many ways errors can arise in them. This task has no margin for error – alter a single atom or bond and you have described a different compound altogether, rendering a promising inhibitor or catalyst inert.

To let scientists focus on hypotheses rather than data wrangling, we built a suite of Cheminformatics Skills for Google Antigravity, turning the surface into a collaborative science workbench with full access to local tooling for both computational chemists and experimental bench chemists.

The skills also create a division of labour where the underlying language model handles workflow orchestration, database navigation and scientific reasoning, while deterministic chemistry tools execute molecular calculations. Grounded in RDKit, the foundational open source cheminformatics toolkit developed over two decades by a global community of contributors, alongside connectors to curated resources from ChEMBL and PubChem, the skills encode domain best practices directly into the agent’s workflow.

Rigidifying a floppy molecule

In this technical walkthrough, we highlight a classic medicinal chemistry technique to improve drug potency: rigidification.

Drug molecules can be complex, often linking multiple substructures through rotatable bonds. Those bonds let the molecule twist into many 3D shapes (conformations), making it “floppy” in solution. To bind its target, however, a floppy molecule must lock into a single active shape. Losing that freedom of motion carries an entropic penalty, which weakens binding affinity and lowers potency.

To solve this problem, medicinal chemists often rigidify molecules by adding new bonds. Two substructures linked by a single rotatable bond can twist into many conformations, but adding a second bond to tie them together sharply limits how they can move in three dimensional space. By placing that bond in the right spot, chemists can lock the molecule into the exact shape needed to bind its target. This strategy, often called “paying the entropic cost in advance”, can dramatically boost potency.

Here, we will focus on a well-known case of successful rigidification. The drug crizotinib is a first-generation, small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) used to treat certain forms of non-small cell lung carcinoma. In crizotinib, there are five major rotatable bonds. Lorlatinib is a third-generation, small-molecule TKI that resembles crizotinib, but has been cyclized at its end to reduce the effective number of rotatable bonds to zero, leading to higher potency among other effects.
